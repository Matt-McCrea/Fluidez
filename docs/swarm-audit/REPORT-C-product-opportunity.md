# Report C: Product Opportunity Audit

Fluidez Spanish course audit, 1 November 2026. For the owner, who will decide what to build.

The lead rulings in `00-lead-rulings.md` bind this report. Where a specialist report disagrees with a ruling, the ruling stands, and section 5 lists what was overruled. R and O numbers refer to that file. No real learner has used any of this, and every minute figure is modelled, not observed (R32).

## 1. The verdict in a page

After every critical repair, Fluidez is a careful Spanish course with a good story that does not lie to you. That beats most of what reviews describe, and it is still not something anyone would go looking for. Every critical finding in the audit repairs trust. Trust is a reason not to leave. It is not a reason to choose.

| The owner asked for | After every critical fix |
|---|---|
| Depth of an excellent grammar course | Close, to about B2. C1 scripts stay "B1 topics with a C1 title" |
| Vocabulary of a sophisticated system | No. About 2,500 words delivered if reserved word time lands, and that is a list, not word knowledge |
| Reading of a graded reader | No. A very good reader with about 82,000 words in it |
| Evidence-based learning | Informed, not proven. No human has been measured |
| Engagement people return to | Not from B1 on. The story is real, and little else is |

**The measured gap.** The innovation review ran the real tagger over real Spanish and asked what share of running words a learner owns. "Taught through B1" is about 1,800 words, close to the 1,822 an average learner actually receives.

| Text | Words | Words a sentence | Taught through B1 | Taught through C1 | Whole graph, 4,554 lexemes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiroga, *Cuentos de amor de locura y de muerte*, 1917 | 16,702 | 15.6 | 83.1% | 85.1% | 89.3% |
| Spanish Wikipedia, La Candelaria | 1,387 | 38.5 | 82.4% | 86.5% | 92.0% |
| El Tiempo, Colombia section, headlines and leads | 417 | 17.4 | 74.2% | 78.1% | 89.2% |
| **Fluidez C1 chapters** | 30,299 | **8.4** | 90.4% | 94.8% | 97.5% |

The course's own C1 reads easier than any real Spanish tried, and one running word in four or five stays unknown in real text. Even the whole graph stops at 89 to 92%. Real-text figures run a point or two low because of lemmatisation, the news sample is small, and Wikipedia's sentence length partly reflects list-like text. The shape is not in doubt, and nothing on the critical list changes it. Reading will not be repaired into existence.

**Why would anyone choose it?** Only if it makes a promise nobody else can make, and keeps it:

> **The Spanish course that ends with you reading a real book, tells you the truth about every sentence you get wrong, and never calls anything yours until you have used it unaided.**

That is a destination, an honest account and honest credit. All three are false today.

The innovation review's wording was "can explain every sentence on the way there". I narrowed it. The final position puts the reason at the miss and the universal panel last, and if misfits are independent, about one panel in four would hold a line that does not fit. A promise in this product has to be true.

The first clause depends on three open decisions: reading volume (O4), document-led B2 and C1 (O5) and a Colombian editor (O6). At today's defaults there is no reading burn and no drafted prose, so the book does not exist. The product then stops at a repaired course with the core in section 4, and the promise should read "ends with you reading real Spanish with help".

One step comes before the largest burn: eight to twelve adults, four weeks, watched. Nobody has done this.

## 2. The ideal application from scratch

The innovation specialist wrote a blank-page design before opening the code, so that it would not be anchored on what exists. Its thesis: language apps measure exposure and completion, while the goal is unprompted, accurate retrieval in sentences the learner has never seen, weeks after practising. So the ideal product optimises one distance, between what a learner can recognise and what they can build unaided.

The table keeps to the standing decisions: text only, no AI at runtime, no speech.

| Area | The ideal | Fluidez today | After the repairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grammar | Short, explicit, contrastive teaching. Choices, not forms, as the core exercise. Scaffolds withdrawn per learner on evidence | 916 plain-language scripts and a ladder that fades support per node. But contrast scripts never reach `teach()`, 0 of 72,693 bank rows target a contrast, and 89 of 94 contrasts appear on one day | Close to the ideal up to about B2. C1 stays thin |
| Vocabulary | Words taught with their grammar and partners, retrieved in unseen sentences, about 4,500 delivered by C1 | 1,822 of 2,528 planned words delivered on the standard tier, including 19 of 380 at B2 and 11 of 332 at C1. No preposition data. 1,265 of 1,830 receptive words never met | A sound list. Word knowledge is a later layer |
| Reading | A reading life: texts ordered by personal coverage, rescue at sentence level, a road to real Spanish | About 82,000 words of one serial. Sentence English on 16 of 11,385 sentences. A third of taps show a note from another sentence | A very good reader with little in it |
| Productive language | Translation as the backbone, target scoring, named errors, connected text, revision | 55 to 61% of cards typed, a real strength. But 5 of 91 valid alternatives accepted, nothing between one sentence and free writing, and writing text discarded | Strong once verdicts are honest. Connected production still missing |
| Personalisation | A diagnostic that returns a map, a record of how each person goes wrong, practice routed from it | A ladder returning one unit number. Error kind computed, then thrown away. Misses do not change what comes next. Saved words never reach the schedule | Logging exists. Routing and map still to build |
| Games and engagement | A story you want to finish, one ritual, proof of progress, personal records, no leagues | Real serial fiction, a forgiving streak, "From last week, X of Y came back". No games, no history of ability, story in 39 of 120 sessions | Story and honesty strong. Little else from B1 |
| Learning environment | Offline, one button, complete look-up, safe data, lessons that survive a broken day | Offline, no account, one button, strong Reference. No backup, resume needs the same day and 3 hours, never run on a phone or Safari, live site six commits behind with no tests in publish | Safe once the first stage lands |

The main finding is this. Fluidez has a generator that refuses when unsure, a tagger that knows why each word is there, and a record of what each person has met. It uses all three to choose the next sentence, and nothing else. The tagger's knowledge is discarded at build time, the checker's diagnosis at answer time, and the learner's own Spanish at the end of each task.

## 3. Competitive position

This section comes from reviews, forums, app-store text and vendors' pages. No competitor was used hands-on. Many review sites are affiliate-funded, and some criticism comes from rivals. The Fluidez side is measured.

| Product | What it does well | Where it falls short | What it means here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo | Habit at scale | Best-known efficacy paper: n=48, about 27 hours, two of three authors staff (abstract). A 2026 course upgrade marked unlearned content as done (press) | Take the warning about rewriting what a learner is assumed to know. R29 exists for this |
| Busuu | Native speakers correct your writing | Feedback covers scripted exercises | A human is the only honest grader of open meaning |
| Kwiziq | A brain map of grammar. The closest rival on diagnosis | Grammar only, quiz-based, nothing on production | It infers weakness from quizzes. Fluidez could infer it from typed sentences |
| Bunpro | Grammar review with nuance hints | "I ended up memorizing the example sentences." | This describes Fluidez's contrast reviews today |
| Satori Reader | Explained reading, sense-correct glosses, one click to review | Hand-made, so scarce | The standard for the reader |
| LingQ, Readlang | Known-word percentage, import of anything, in-context help | Buggy, weak status tracking | Take the percentage and the paste |
| Language Transfer | Thousands of sentences built from bridges out of English | One person, testimonial evidence, audio | Fluidez teaches no transfer from English |
| Math Academy, Khan | Diagnostic as a profile, honest skill levels | No formal studies of Math Academy's method. Khan's effects appear for roughly the 5% who use it as prescribed (Holt 2024, abstract) | Adherence may be the binding constraint |

**Where Fluidez is already better:**

- **A strict claim of knowing.** A word sticks only with long stability and a pass in a sentence never seen. No competitor examined has a definition this strict.
- **Generators that refuse.** Once transforms are licensed, round-tripping every form through the analyser is a promise an LLM product cannot make.
- **Production load.** 55 to 61% of cards typed, a quarter to a third whole sentences.
- **Knowing what a sentence needs.** Every row carries what it requires, so the app can say what a learner has been taught at every word.
- **Support withdrawn per node on evidence**, and bounded partial credit.
- **Tone.** No leagues, no hearts, and "That's plenty for today."

**Where it is behind:** reading supply (LingQ, Satori), diagnosis (Kwiziq), habit machinery (Duolingo), human feedback (Busuu), and everything spoken, by decision.

**The opening.** Products with explanation have little practice. Products with practice have little explanation. A deterministic system that can account for its sentences and remember how a person goes wrong had no direct competitor in about 50 searches. Absence from searches is not proof, and the claim about LLM explanation in rival apps is from memory. Treat this as a strong impression.

## 4. The distinctive core, and what to say no to

The core is three things, with honest credit running through all of them.

1. **The sentence accounts for itself.** A true reason at the miss, named traps, and what the learner avoids. The engine and tagger turned to face the learner.
2. **The choice is the practice.** One contrast store, used everywhere, in a format set by what decides each contrast (R10), because choices are what defeat English speakers.
3. **A world worth reading your way through.** The serial, then the plot's own documents, a real book, real Spanish beyond it, and the learner's own Second Part. This third part needs O4, O5 and O6.

**Say no to:**

- A shelf of generic graded passages, and word-count targets. Gate the meetings. The total is an output.
- Rewriting the chapters (R26).
- Any new block that does not name what it replaces (R20).
- A second daily ritual.
- More than three public measures of progress.
- Points by another name, including levels built on assumed difficulty (R22, R23).
- CEFR labels as claims (R24).
- Any number that has not passed the tests in section 6.
- Say-it-aloud, in any form.
- Lesson-length choice. The app already breaks this rule twice: "Only five minutes?" at `home.tsx:79` and the tier radio in Settings. Both should go.
- Drafted prose no Colombian has read (O6).
- Social features before there is anyone to be social with.

### Valuable missing capabilities

#### 1. Keep what the learner makes

- **Problem:** Up to 19 months of learning live in one browser, with no export. Writing text and the checker's diagnosis are thrown away.
- **Why it matters:** A lost phone ends the course, and data not logged today cannot be recovered. R27 puts this before every game.
- **How it works:** Export and import one JSON file, restored through migrations. Persistent storage. Writing under its own key. A separate capped attempts log with the R17 fields. Reading rows and a silent monthly snapshot.
- **Acquisition:** None directly. Every personal feature here reads these logs.
- **Fit:** Own keys, nothing on `ReviewEvent`. First stage of the architecture plan.
- **Evaluation:** A golden backup restores with reps intact on a second device.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice for a portfolio. No learning claim for the rest.
- **Minutes:** 0.

#### 2. Contrast practice shaped by what decides the contrast

- **Problem:** The 94 contrasts sit outside practice. A due one falls to a bare card, or a read card that grades Good with no retrieval.
- **Why it matters:** English speakers still get these choices wrong at B2, and a voluntary game is no return for anyone who does not play.
- **How it works:** Teach days keep the full script (R21). One `ContrastItem` store (R11). Format by class (R10): 30 local-trigger contrasts get a binary gap, then typing. 27 meaning contrasts get twins and both sides typed. 8 discourse contrasts get connected gaps and translation, no clock. 29 near-synonyms get phrase retrieval, no decks. Start at 40 items, 150 for the big five.
- **Acquisition:** Puts discrimination on the across-session schedule, where interleaving helped on Spanish tense (Pan et al. 2019; one replication found no difference).
- **Fit:** No new `Card` member, no `x:` ids on existing rows. Script fixes ship in the same release (R5). The 1,471 existing script items can rotate before any burn.
- **Evaluation:** Delayed typed accuracy on contrast nodes. Cue Test in CI.
- **Evidence grade:** Strong research support, not unanimous. The class split is a reasonable but uncertain hypothesis, from one rater.
- **Minutes:** About 0 net in Warm up: 5 to 8% of cards, up from 1.7%, replacing bare recall. A teach day moves `communicative` and freer `writing` off, recovering about 6 of 6.6 minutes (R21), still to be simulated.

#### 3. A reader that works and can simply be opened

- **Problem:** 32.2% of tapped tokens show a note from another sentence, and a learner placed at u45 reads chapter 1.
- **Why it matters:** With no listening, reading is the only input channel.
- **How it works:** A phrase note shows only if it covers the tapped token. A table for *del, al, hay*. Lemma and form per token after the re-tag. Sentence English behind a tap. An open library: next chapter, everything at or below level, the story so far, known share per text. Chapters never wait (R26).
- **Acquisition:** Glossing helps comprehension and vocabulary (general finding from memory; Yun 2011 in excerpt only).
- **Fit:** `bundle.gloss`, a derived `library.json`. Taps stay free (R25).
- **Evaluation:** An all-chapters test that no token gets a note absent from its sentence.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice.
- **Minutes:** 0 in the lesson.

#### 4. What English already gives you (Puentes)

- **Problem:** "Cognate" appears once in 916 scripts, while about 20 to 38% of lexemes are rough cognates.
- **Why it matters:** It is the cheapest large vocabulary gain available.
- **How it works:** Five small A1 lessons on rules such as *-tion* to *-ción*: guess, then read. Cognates take a receptive fast path. False friends become named traps. Word families are recognition only (R7).
- **Acquisition:** Cognate awareness improved vocabulary in several studies, mostly on Spanish speakers learning English.
- **Fit:** New nodes placed by hand, in the one re-tag (R30).
- **Evaluation:** An unseen-cognate recognition test at week 2.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice, mostly from the other language direction.
- **Minutes:** Negative. The fast path sits in the lever table. Lessons replace read-and-pick in Practise.

#### 5. Words with their grammar

- **Problem:** A learner can know *confiar* and write *confiar a*. Lexemes hold no preposition or partner data.
- **Why it matters:** Much advanced error is lexical grammar.
- **How it works:** Mine partners at build time and curate them: *confiar en* 61 of 65 uses. One strip on the word card and in Look up. Practise whole phrases, never by matching.
- **Acquisition:** Intact phrases did best at delayed post-test (Boers et al. 2014 and a partial replication, abstracts).
- **Fit:** Generated, gated fields in the one re-tag. Not 600 to 800 new cards.
- **Evaluation:** Preposition errors per opportunity in the attempts log.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice.
- **Minutes:** 0.

#### 6. Fixed instruments

- **Problem:** Everything inside practice measures practice. The app cannot tell a memorised sentence from a rule.
- **Why it matters:** Nothing can be validated without it, and R16 holds any mastery number until probes exist.
- **How it works:** Quarantined transfer probes at 28 and 90 days, silent. A form of a regular verb the learner has never met (Berko 1958, from memory). Monthly, rotating: Remiendo, a C-test on fixed passages, and a cold read.
- **Acquisition:** A delayed test in a new sentence is itself retrieval (Butler 2010).
- **Fit:** The gate forbids probe rows in practice. Needs `ContrastItem` and `focus`.
- **Evaluation:** Colombian speakers ace each passage, and Remiendo tracks the cold read.
- **Evidence grade:** Strong research support for the principle. Novel idea requiring experimentation as a metric.
- **Minutes:** Under 1 a session, taking a card's place in Warm up. Monthly measures sit outside lessons.

#### 7. The tutor brief

- **Problem:** A finisher may still be unable to follow a fast taxi driver in Bogotá.
- **Why it matters:** The app should say so plainly and hand the learner on.
- **How it works:** A printable page of what holds, what is shaky, three traps and ten sentences to drill, offered once.
- **Acquisition:** Indirect. It targets speaking time bought elsewhere.
- **Fit:** An export, after traps exist.
- **Evaluation:** Do testers use it?
- **Evidence grade:** Novel idea requiring experimentation.
- **Minutes:** 0.

### New learning experiences

#### 8. Connected verb gaps, then marked lines

- **Problem:** Nothing sits between one sentence and free writing. A full rebuild fails: 0 of 24 valid rebuilds passed the matcher, so only exact recall would score.
- **Why it matters:** Aspect is decided by context. "Always preterite" scores 82% on natural bank sentences (the known default, Salaberry, from memory), so isolated choices train the wrong habit.
- **How it works:** A2 up: a chapter passage returns with verbs as infinitives, and the learner types the forms, about 7 past-tense decisions per 100 words. An `also` list holds true alternatives. B1 up: one or two lines from English, scored on the marked span. B2 up: narration beats compared side by side, exposure only.
- **Acquisition:** Output makes learners notice gaps (Izumi 2002, abstract, task design unconfirmed).
- **Fit:** R19. Later stages need `focus` and chapter English. Must not eat re-reads at A2.
- **Evaluation:** Delayed production of target nodes against plain reread, then on new sentences.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice in classrooms. Novel idea requiring experimentation at this scale.
- **Minutes:** 2, about one day in three, replacing remix in Practise. At A2 it takes that day's Use slot.

#### 9. Evidence questions

- **Problem:** Questions can be passed by picking the longest option, 88 to 92% from B1, and many are plot recall.
- **Why it matters:** Upper-band reading turns on what a sentence commits the speaker to.
- **How it works:** Questions only the form can answer. *No quiso firmar* or *No quería firmar*: could the person have signed anyway? Gate rules: the right option is longest at most 30% of the time, and the answer cannot be reached with the statement removed. El Caso's frame is a five-case pilot only.
- **Acquisition:** Form-meaning mapping under a comprehension task (VanPatten and Cadierno 1993; superiority disputed). It does not replace production.
- **Fit:** Uses `ContrastItem`. Spanish examples need a native reader.
- **Evaluation:** Fresh contrast items two weeks later.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice. The case frame is a novel idea requiring experimentation.
- **Minutes:** About 2 in Read, replacing plot-recall questions.

#### 10. Story in every session, and the hook first

- **Problem:** Only 39 of 120 sessions from zero had a chapter or a scene. Day 1 reaches the chapter at minute 14. The story card starts at 1,093 px with 787 visible.
- **Why it matters:** The story is the main retention asset, and it is rationed and off the first screen.
- **How it works:** Chapters run in beats through `splitBeats`. Day 1 opens on three lines at a door in Bogotá, teaches what the next lines need, and returns. Scenes stop waiting on owed words. Today shows the hook, Start and one quiet line.
- **Acquisition:** Daily meaning-focused input. Chunks arrive because the next line needs them.
- **Fit:** No new Spanish beyond beat-end lines. Pilot on parts 1 to 5.
- **Evaluation:** Whether testers ask for the next beat. Lessons per week.
- **Evidence grade:** Reasonable but uncertain hypothesis.
- **Minutes:** About 1 more in Read at A1 (2.5 against 1.6), paid by a 15-card warm-up, which needs the owner's call on 5 carded items a day.

#### 11. Write, compare, rewrite, and fix your own errors

- **Problem:** Writing ends at one attempt, 212 words of nonsense earned three of four ticks, and 2,933 authored pitfalls go unused at runtime.
- **Why it matters:** Self-editing and syntactic complexity need their own practice.
- **How it works:** From B2: read the model, mark what it does that you did not, rewrite once from memory, keep it. Lint only at 95% precision, never scored. From B1: the learner's own errors, then filtered pitfalls, always retyped. The owner must approve showing wrong Spanish. This replaces Simón dice.
- **Acquisition:** Models help learners notice new problems (Hanaoka and Izumi 2012, abstract). Correcting one's own error is a strong moment (Butterfield and Metcalfe 2001, from memory), and wrong forms can harm (Jacoby and Hollingshead 1990, from memory), hence the retype.
- **Fit:** Needs saved writing and the attempts log. Fails open on free text.
- **Evaluation:** The same prompt each quarter: clauses per sentence, tense range, lint errors per 100 words.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice for revision. Reasonable but uncertain hypothesis for retyping errors.
- **Minutes:** Takes that day's Use slot, 3.5 at B1. Nothing over 250 words in a lesson. Retyping takes at most 1 in Practise, replacing focused gaps.

### Engagement mechanisms

#### 12. Four games, offered when they open

- **Problem:** The rebuild has no games, and its one score rarely compares like with like.
- **Why it matters:** The honest case is dose. The course gives 242 hours against 700 to 800 guided hours for C1 (English figures, so rough).
- **How it works:** Section 6.
- **Acquisition:** Practice on known material (Nation 2007; DeKeyser 1997). Gamification effects are small (Sailer and Homner 2020). Games are not a teaching method.
- **Fit:** `src/games` touches no bottleneck file. After backup (R27). Until O11 is decided, games arrive through Close offers and the Practise card.
- **Evaluation:** Lessons per week do not fall, and calm review load stays flat.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice for fluency on known material. Reasonable but uncertain hypothesis for typed sprints.
- **Minutes:** 0 in the lesson, by rule. Voluntary time outside it.

#### 13. Landmarks: band letters and a monthly page

- **Problem:** A 19-month path has no landmarks or history of ability, and "You can now" ticks by position: a learner placed at u30 sees thirty ticks on day one.
- **Why it matters:** Adults stay for competence they can verify, and B1's 14 units are where gains go invisible.
- **How it works:** Can-dos tick by evidence. At each stage end, a letter from a character in exactly that stage's Spanish, unglossed, with three questions. "Vale wrote to you. You can read this now." One monthly page: the same text then and now, early writing beside newest, one optional measure at most.
- **Acquisition:** Delayed retrieval hidden in the letter. None claimed for the page.
- **Fit:** Five authored letters, so O6 applies. Stage labels (R24). Every number passes section 6's tests.
- **Evaluation:** Do testers finish the letter and return the next day?
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice for delayed checks. Reasonable but uncertain hypothesis for the rest.
- **Minutes:** About 12 once per stage in Read, replacing that day's reading. One optional measure of 3 to 8 minutes a month, outside lessons.

#### 14. A return without blame

- **Problem:** Old debt stops new teaching. At the app's own item budgets, a learner placed at u45 spent 19 of 21 sessions in review only. A returning learner meets about 61 cards at real pace (the 490 figure was largely a bot artefact).
- **Why it matters:** Absence is certain on a 19-month course, and people drop off once a logged streak breaks (Silverman and Barasch 2023, abstract).
- **How it works:** A compiler debt policy pays old debt at a capped rate without throttling teaching (R18). After 7 or more days: at most 10 minutes, 8 high-value cards, five lines of "Previously", then the next beat of the story. No figure and no streak talk (R22).
- **Acquisition:** Reviews stay spaced, and fewer false lapses follow a break.
- **Fit:** `plan.reentry`, no new `LessonType`. Touches no card, and it is reversible.
- **Evaluation:** Absence and placed profiles in the simulator, required before it ships.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice.
- **Minutes:** Replaces the lesson on the return day, capped at 10.

### Personalisation systems

#### 15. Five-minute placement, calibrated in use, then a map

- **Problem:** The ladder returns one number and assumes everything earlier is known. The likeliest adult here is a false beginner with patchy knowledge.
- **Why it matters:** A wrong seed wastes months or demoralises.
- **How it works:** About 15 hand-built items, at most 4 typed. The first five lessons calibrate, with visible "Too easy" and "Too hard". A failed skill stays unseeded and is owed, capped. A map shows four states from existing data: Met, Can choose it, Can build it, Holds up in new sentences.
- **Acquisition:** Indirect, through targeting.
- **Fit:** Late, after the contrast store and acceptance, because a typed item on today's checker would fail the learners it is meant to find. No level tied to course position (R22).
- **Evaluation:** Lapse rate on seeded nodes in the first 30 days.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice.
- **Minutes:** Five, once. Calibration runs inside ordinary lessons.

#### 16. Misses that come back, and a Train it that trains

- **Problem:** A miss in controlled output changes nothing. Train it on por and para can land on a verb drill or "week 165".
- **Why it matters:** The learning is in routed practice, not in viewing a list.
- **How it works:** Up to three misses return in a fresh sentence. Train it routes by error kind to choice drills on the actual contrast. Weak spots count Assert hits only (R14). Free-form misses locate, and training stays by form. A traps screen comes later, above a 95% precision bar, three at most, led by what has stopped happening.
- **Acquisition:** Retrieval across sessions is well supported (Karpicke and Roediger 2008). Routing is untested.
- **Fit:** Attempts log, then traps, then routing.
- **Evaluation:** Recurrence per opportunity over 30 days, before and after, on the owner's logs.
- **Evidence grade:** Strong research support for returning misses. Reasonable but uncertain hypothesis for routing.
- **Minutes:** 1 to 2 inside Warm up, within its cap, so older reviews wait. Train it is voluntary.

#### 17. Saved words, and vocabulary pulled by the reading ahead

- **Problem:** Saved words never reach the schedule, and owed words arrive in plan order, which rank errors corrupt.
- **Why it matters:** The words a learner cares about most are the only ones the memory model ignores.
- **How it works:** A save creates a card with its sentence, at most three a day (R25). In the owed queue, A2 and B1 serve words by frequency in the next 30 chapters, and B2 and up weight real-corpus frequency too. For 200 words at u28, chapters ahead reach 96.2% against 93.7% in plan order.
- **Acquisition:** Deliberate study of a word met in context. Pull raises coverage of the next text.
- **Fit:** Needs reserved word time and the rank rebuild.
- **Evaluation:** Retention of saved words. Known share of the next chapters.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice.
- **Minutes:** A carded item costs about 4.7 minutes over 18 months, so a save takes a slot from New and is never added on top. Reading pull costs 0.

### Distinctive ideas

#### 18. Named traps at the miss

- **Problem:** *Si tendría dinero* gets "when does it happen?". Hints mislead on the hardest grammar. Three batteries disagree on rates (0 of 30 named, 18 of 50 useful, 46 of 249), so quote none. They agree on direction.
- **Why it matters:** This is when the learner works hardest, and a false reason reinforces the error.
- **How it works:** One module, `src/check/traps/`, merging the two rule tables. The 2,933 pitfalls scoped to the card's nodes (unscoped: 20 hits, 5 wrong). Four outcomes: Assert, Ask, Note, Silent (R14). A word-level diff, and one line pushed at the miss with "See the rule". A correct-answer battery must produce no Assert.
- **Acquisition:** Prompts beat recasts, clearest on free responses (Lyster and Saito 2010, abstract; a later replication found no such advantage).
- **Fit:** Honest hints, then acceptance, then traps, or a trap explains away a right answer.
- **Evaluation:** Batteries as a regression suite: 0 misleading. Recurrence on the owner's logs.
- **Evidence grade:** Strong research support for prompts and feedback.
- **Minutes:** About 0 net. About 6 seconds a sentence miss, or +0.5 to +0.8 a session with the diff and verdict, paid by honest verdicts.

#### 19. Why?, in three parts

- **Problem:** "Why this form here?" is the adult's most common question, and the app answers with a lesson about a rule.
- **Why it matters:** Depth is the owner's first objective. But the universal panel ranks low per learner-minute.
- **How it works:** Part one is the pushed line in proposal 18. Part two is "Why this form?" in the reader's tap panel: anatomy only, from a whitelist of feature families. Part three, a panel on any sentence, waits until acquisition's top eight land, and every opening then ends in a retrieval.
- **Acquisition:** Feedback at the error works. A panel costs 20 to 30 seconds, about one translation card, and pull help tends to reach those who need it least (Aleven et al. 2003, from memory).
- **Fit:** A lazy tagger chunk, 146 KB gzip and 536 KB raw, not the 82 KB in the innovation report, which counted data files only. Plus a small override table, after the re-tag. Needed anyway because learner text cannot be precomputed.
- **Evaluation:** A hand sample of reader anatomy with no false statements. When learners exist: under 5% of misses opening it, or under 3 points' gain, keeps the panel out.
- **Evidence grade:** Strong research support at the miss. Novel idea requiring experimentation as a panel.
- **Minutes:** Pushed line inside proposal 18. Reader use costs seconds in Read. The panel is voluntary, never a lesson detour.

#### 20. The app turns into Spanish as you learn it

- **Problem:** The reader at C1 is still an English experience. 215 of 219 hooks and 77% of questions are in English.
- **Why it matters:** It would be the most frequent comprehensible input in the app, and the app would address you only in Spanish it knows you own.
- **How it works:** Each UI string gets a Spanish twin and flips when its `requires` are known. Tap for English, always. Destructive actions never flip. Hooks stay English through B1. Start with forty of about 670 strings.
- **Acquisition:** Repeated meaningful input in context. Unproven in this form.
- **Fit:** Gate-checkable. Owner decision, and the strings need a native reader (O6).
- **Evaluation:** Watch testers. Tap-for-English rate per string should fall.
- **Evidence grade:** Novel idea requiring experimentation.
- **Minutes:** 0.

#### 21. What you never do

- **Problem:** Traps catch errors. Nothing catches avoidance. A learner can pass every drill on the imperfect subjunctive and never choose it.
- **Why it matters:** Avoidance hides where accuracy looks fine.
- **How it works:** Once writing is stored, the tagger reports on the learner's text: "You have been taught *aunque* with the subjunctive and have never chosen it." That sets the constraint on the next writing task. Signal only.
- **Acquisition:** Avoidance is a classic finding (Schachter 1974; Laufer and Eliasson 1993, both from memory).
- **Fit:** Needs saved writing and the runtime tagger. Blind for 37 grammar nodes, and absence is not proof.
- **Evaluation:** Use of the named structure on the quarterly same-prompt task.
- **Evidence grade:** Novel idea requiring experimentation.
- **Minutes:** 0. It changes the constraint on an existing Use task.

### Ambitious ideas

These four need O4, O5 or O6. At today's defaults none is built.

#### 22. Document lessons at B2 and C1

- **Problem:** Can-dos promise news and institutional Spanish, and the news text at u45 is about 40 words. Everything quoted across the serial totals 6,194 words, while the plot depends on 47 letters and 32 notebooks the learner never reads.
- **Why it matters:** Each upper-band proposal is right alone and a pile together. One lesson shape absorbs them.
- **How it works:** At u45: personal gap work, Mariana's front page in about 650 words, eight evidence questions, ten contrast items, one statement translated line by line, a short correction note, then ch142 as the reward. Existing chapters stay (R26). The app says plainly this is a made-up paper.
- **Acquisition:** Reading, translation and writing tied to one text, with genre range the course lacks.
- **Fit:** Needs stable day keys and `reconcile` (R29), and parts that survive midnight. The lesson is fixed, and the learner chooses when to stop.
- **Evaluation:** One unit, end to end, played by three strong learners beside today's u45.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice for reading-led days. Novel idea requiring experimentation as the whole shape.
- **Minutes:** About 48 a lesson in parts. That figure assumed say-it cards, so expect more. By my arithmetic, 150 such lessons and today's 190 days at about 38 modelled minutes both come to about 120 hours, so it replaces the upper-band day rather than adding hours.

#### 23. Fares: Hernando's notebooks

- **Problem:** 1,265 receptive words are never met, 92% of C1 words appear in fewer than 3 later chapters, and first-pass reading is about 5% of course time.
- **Why it matters:** The research supports meetings, not totals, and a generic shelf is a commodity. A fare stands alone, so placed learners have something at their level from day one.
- **How it works:** One fare, one small story, one a day, from the notebooks the plot names. Graded by the notebook's year, so a terse A1 log is natural. 30 first, then 300, then 1,000. Gate recycling and sentence length, not totals.
- **Acquisition:** Extensive reading has moderate support (Nakanishi 2015, abstract), and per-word gains are low (Waring and Takaki 2003, from memory). Running stories reduce vocabulary load (Hwang and Nation 1989; Schmitt and Carter 2000, both from memory).
- **Fit:** A `ReadingPiece` type after the lexeme rebuild. Read-only days count for the streak.
- **Evaluation:** A Colombian editor reads the first 30. Cold-read rate rises.
- **Evidence grade:** Strong research support for direction, with modest per-word gains. Novel idea requiring experimentation as a format.
- **Minutes:** 3 to 5 in Read. At 300,000 words: 1.7 at A1, 4.3 at B1, 7.2 at C1. Replaces extra reading and the same-session re-read. The rest must come from honest verdicts, or it does not fit.

#### 24. The book, and Second Part

- **Problem:** Twenty months end with "You've finished the course. Reviews keep it all fresh."
- **Why it matters:** People choose outcomes, and forgive a tool for being hard if they believe in where it goes.
- **How it works:** Hernando's memoir as a real book, 60,000 to 70,000 words of prose. The first page sits in Read from week 1, each word lit by whether the learner owns it. ch204 ends with Tomás writing «Segunda parte», and the learner's saved writing belongs there.
- **Acquisition:** Sustained reading at real length, with vocabulary already met in the notebooks.
- **Fit:** Only the few reach C1 (Holt 2024), so the first page and fares must arrive in week one. Needs O4 and O6.
- **Evaluation:** An editor reads the first three chapters.
- **Evidence grade:** Novel idea requiring experimentation.
- **Minutes:** About 6 to 7 hours at an assumed 170 words a minute, in Read at C1 or as free reading.

#### 25. Real Spanish: prepare for it, paste it, measure against it

- **Problem:** The app has no exit, and `requires` only asks whether a learner may see a text, never what it would take to read it.
- **Why it matters:** It is the only honest yardstick, and an adult's most ordinary reason is a contract on Friday. The innovation review raises it to core at B2, while acquisition and product place it late. I follow the later placing, because at 78 to 86% coverage real text is unusable until late.
- **How it works:** Text minus known gives a set of nodes, and the compiler can serve that set: "9 words and one structure short of reading this cold". In-app text first. Then paste a text and see known share, glosses, anatomy and words to save. Fixed real texts form a yardstick: public-domain work, Spanish Wikipedia, official Colombian text.
- **Acquisition:** Self-chosen reading with targeted preparation. Low value until about C1, and no direct evidence for the preparation.
- **Fit:** `compileSession` is pure, so a prepared lesson is a plan like any other, not a length choice. Paste needs a 30,000-lemma gloss list and degrades to anatomy, because 43 to 56% of real-text sentences come back low confidence. Rights checked per text.
- **Evaluation:** Tap rate on prepared against unprepared texts. Known share on the fixed texts rising over months.
- **Evidence grade:** Established pedagogical practice for paste, copied from LingQ and Readlang. Reasonable but uncertain hypothesis for preparation.
- **Minutes:** Preparation replaces New on that day, inside the same budget. Paste is 0 in the lesson. Real pieces can take a Read slot at C1.

## 6. Games in detail

The rebuild has no games. The old arcade must not be ported: blind tapping beat knowledge, its rating fell for a learner whose skill never changed, and its daily was not the same for everyone.

### Which, and in what order

These are the four in R23, in the order their dependencies allow.

| # | Game | What it trains | Round | Score | Touches FSRS | Waits for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relámpago | Verb form from meaning, typed, resolved the moment it is right | 60 to 90 s | Forms right per fixed deck | No | Backup, game shell |
| 2 | Rescate | What FSRS says is due. No clock, "I don't know" allowed | 3 to 5 min | Cards done | Yes, hits and misses | Backup |
| 3 | Uno u otro | The decision between two real forms | 60 to 90 s | Pairs right per fixed deck | No | Contrast store, Cue Test |
| 4 | De corrido | Reading known text fast, with comprehension gated | 2 to 3 min | Words a minute at the comprehension shown | No | Reading rows, question gate |
| Later | El tinto | One sentence a day, six tries, with a "right verb, wrong ending" mark | 2 to 4 min | Tries used | No | Target scoring (R23) |

**Why this order.** Relámpago needs no content and builds the shell. A cell enters speed work only after about 90% calm accuracy over ten attempts, because rehearsing a shaky form fast can strengthen the wrong one. Rescate is an end screen on existing code, and the only game that grades real cards.

Uno u otro starts with the 30 local-trigger contrasts, where matching the cue is the skill. Meaning decks come later, twins only. Discourse contrasts are never timed. Near-synonyms get no deck.

De corrido is the only game aimed at reading. El tinto waits because a daily that rejects correct Spanish does so to every user at once. It carries no learning claim, because the marks allow hill-climbing.

**Changes to the games report's specifications:**

- **De corrido's gate does not work yet.** It discards a run with under two of three right. By my arithmetic, someone who reads nothing and picks the longest option passes about 68% of the time at B1, 83% at B2 and 86% at C1 on today's re-read questions, assuming independent questions. It waits for the option-length gate rule.
- **Rescate loses its recall bar and its hook.** A per-card recall chance is a learner-facing memory number R16 does not yet allow, and "These are about to slip away" sits badly with R22.
- **Rereads in the lesson are unresolved.** Acquisition wants a timed reread protected inside the lesson, and product wants it in the game above A2. If it stays, the 0.7 and 1.1 minutes in section 5's lever table are not available.

**What stays from the brief.** Short activities: rounds of 60 to 90 seconds, playable one-handed at a bus stop. High scores: a personal best on the tile, best today, and a ghost of your own best run. More demanding challenges: Uno u otro's rungs (meaning cue, gap with English, gap alone), an Everything deck, subjunctive decks in Relámpago, and De corrido's weekly long run on C1's 1,000-word chapters.

### The rules that keep them honest

Every number must pass six tests, automated where possible. Five are from the games report, the sixth from R10.

| Test | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Idle | Play twice as much without improving: does the number rise? If so, it is XP | |
| Guess | Can someone who cannot read Spanish beat someone who can? | Old Uno u otro: blind tapper 10,168, quick knower 6,975 |
| Repeat | Can you raise it by replaying the same easy thing? | Needs a fixed deck |
| Rust | If you stop being able to do it, does a round show that? | The round shows it. No standing number falls (R22) |
| Plain sentence | Can you say what it means in one sentence? | "41 forms in a minute" passes. "8,420 points" does not |
| Cue | Does a cue table score at most 65%, and always-commoner at most 55%, on meaning and discourse decks? | Always-preterite beat a careful knower 23.5 to 14.5 under the recommended clock |

**The clock.** Start at 60 seconds. A wrong answer costs time at once, then the clock stops while the correction is on screen. With no penalty, a blind 0.3-second tapper makes 34.6 rights in 60 seconds, ahead of a 90% knower at 3 seconds (18.3). With a 6-second penalty the tapper falls to 7.9 and the knower holds 15.4. With a free correction, the penalty is about 7 seconds on two-option items and 3 on typed ones.

The clock beats blind tapping. It does not beat a good rule of thumb, which is why the Cue Test and balanced decks exist: on a balanced deck a quick cue-table player still ties a careful knower, 14.6 to 14.5. Speed gets no learning claim.

**The other rules.**

- Backup, saved writing and the attempts log land first (R27).
- Only due-card, no-clock play touches FSRS (R23). In a 540-day simulation, full credit for hits at any time dropped recall at review to 0.887 against a 0.90 target, and counting hits and misses at any time raised calm review load 46%. These rest on an assumed trace strength, so trust the order, not the numbers.
- `ms` never reaches `recordTime` (R23), with a test, because that timing drives every budget.
- Personal bests live inside one game, per fixed deck, in natural units. No app-wide currency (R23).
- Games log on the item's target node only. One event per required node would add about 390,000 events to the 365-day window.
- Speed games use closed answers only. `matchTranslation` accepted 95 of 8,747 adjacent word swaps, and at least 20 of the 70 read were ungrammatical.
- Games never advance the path, count for the streak or appear in lesson minutes.
- No locked outlines (R23). A game arrives as an offer when it opens: "Sixty seconds on this, now?"
- No line compares a game with lesson position (R22).
- One input that never unmounts, so the keyboard stays up. Not yet tested on an iPhone.

The kill metric is lessons per week. If games raise play and lower lessons, they have failed, however good they feel.

Acquisition's power figures show when arms become worth reopening: one learner on a paired split has 0.26 power for a 5-point gain, and five learners have 0.93. Never hold out fixes.

Kill rules marked * are proposed here. The others come from the specialist reports or rulings.

| # | Experiment | How | Kill rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Watched adults | Eight to twelve English speakers, four weeks, after Stage 2 | * If under half reach week 4 unprompted, stop the largest burn and fix what they hit |

## 10. What this audit could not establish

- **Whether any of this teaches anyone.** No real learner, no phone, no Safari. Simulations take true memory from FSRS itself, so they cannot show learning.
- **Who it is for.** If this is the owner's app for a few people, much of the sharing and competition work is moot. If it is public, distribution is the largest missing feature. Nobody has decided.
- **Seconds per typed sentence on a phone.** The most valuable number the project lacks.
- **The real false-rejection rate.** The 5 to 20% in the simulations is assumed.
- **Whether the proposed prose would be any good.** No fare, document or book chapter exists. No human Colombian reader has reviewed any of the content, and 10 verdicts await a native check.
- **Whether the fixes fit.** The debt policy (R18) and the contrast-day swap (R21) are designs, not simulated results. My budget table is arithmetic on modelled figures.
- **The tagger on a real device.** Timing is from Node, first-call cost is unmeasured, and the 146 KB excludes graph data already in core.
- **Span quality.** A span on 95.4% of grammar rows shows a feature exists there, not that it is the right decision to gap (R12).
- **Competitors at first hand.** Searches found nothing usable on ¡Dímelo tú!, Gramática de uso or Butt and Benjamin.
- **Pragmatics.** Never audited. A correct, blunt sentence can do more harm in Bogotá than a wrong tense.
- **Sync.** Export is backup, not sync, and whether a small sync service is acceptable is an open question for the owner.
- **Rights for real texts.** They need checking per text, including Mistral.
- **Single-rater judgements.** The 94-contrast classification, the 59-feature hand check and the hint batteries each had one judge. Cue tables are ceilings.

**Small disagreements left in the sources.** The serial is 82,164 words in one count and 87,034 tokens in another. Twin sets in contrast scripts are 81 or 82. Neither changes a conclusion.

**Checked directly for this report.** `navigator.storage` has 0 uses in `app/src`, so the games report's claim that `app.tsx` requests persistence is wrong. `SESSION_MINUTES` still reads 28 and 30 for B2 and C1. "Only five minutes?" and the Settings tier radio exist. `decisions.md` records the removal of "say it aloud". All other figures come from the specialist reports and were not re-run.

**Citations from memory, unverified.** Web search budgets ran out during the cross-reviews, so these stay marked and should be checked before anyone quotes them: Aleven et al. 2003; Berko 1958; Butterfield and Metcalfe 2001; Hwang and Nation 1989; Jacoby and Hollingshead 1990; Laufer and Eliasson 1993; Salaberry on the default past; Schachter 1974; Schmitt and Carter 2000; Waring and Takaki 2003; the general benefit of glosses; LLM explanation in rival apps. Sources seen only as abstracts or secondary reports are marked where cited, and full status is in the reference sections of `06-innovation.md`, `06-games-and-engagement.md` and `03-acquisition.md`.
