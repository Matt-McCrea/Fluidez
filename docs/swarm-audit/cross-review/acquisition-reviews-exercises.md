# Cross-review: would the proposed exercises produce learning?

Reviewer: Second-Language Acquisition Specialist. Date: 1 November 2026. Reviews `06-innovation.md`, `06-games-and-engagement.md`, the recommendations in `02-contrastive.md`, `05-product-journeys-lower.md` and `05-product-journeys-upper.md`, and my own `03-acquisition.md`.

Evidence tiers follow my report: **Strong** (strong research support), **Established** (established practice), **Hypothesis** (reasonable, uncertain), **Novel** (needs experiment).

## The short answer

The best learning gains on the table are not new features. They sit in the minutes learners already spend. At an honest pace the warm-up is 40 to 54% of card time, and nearly every high-value change acts there or at the moment of a miss. New exercises rank below those fixes because they buy their minutes from somewhere.

On the proposals I was asked to challenge:

- **Binary choice builds the decision only for some contrasts.** For 30 of 94 contrasts a nearby word really does decide, so matching the cue is the skill. For aspect it is close to the opposite: "always preterite" scores 82% on natural sentences and, on a deck at natural frequency, beats a careful learner under the games report's recommended clock (23.5 against 14.5). The Guess Test cannot see this. A new Cue Test can.
- **Reconstruye does not work as written.** All 24 valid reconstructions of real chapter lines that I put through the real matcher were rejected, each with a false reason. A smaller form of it, verbs gapped in a passage from the chapter, is one of my top ten.
- **Rayos X as a universal panel is overrated.** The part that earns its minutes is a reason pushed at the moment of a miss. The panel on every sentence is the weakest of the "four distinctive ideas".
- **A self-grade must never earn Good.** Good lets recall slip to or below the 0.90 target for a typical overclaiming learner, and to 0.85 under gaming. Any of three lesser credits removes most of the harm. I recommend no credit plus a retest in a fresh sentence.
- **500,000 words of reading does not fit the course.** That was my number and it was wrong for a fixed-length lesson. About 250,000 to 300,000 new words does fit, and it is about what the vocabulary needs.
- **Evidence weighting by format, as I proposed it, should be dropped.** It raises review load 63% and lastingly distorts difficulty, for a benefit confined to a card that matters little. A mastery estimate should run silently until a transfer probe shows it predicts anything.

Nothing here rests on real learners, because none exist. Sections 3 to 11 separate what I measured from what I judged.

## 2. What to measure from day one

No telemetry, no server and no AI are needed. The design is an on-device log, the JSON export already proposed for backup, and a small analysis script run by the owner on files people choose to send him. The owner is the first subject. The unit of analysis is the node, not the person, which is why very few learners can still show something.

**A. Capture now. Data not logged today cannot be recovered.**

| Field | Where | Why it cannot wait |
|---|---|---|
| `fmt` (the card format actually delivered) | Every event | `ReviewEvent` has `op` but no format, so every warm-up card is just `warmup`. Whether a cloze pass predicts anything differently from a translation pass cannot be tested today |
| `given`, `rule` | Misses | Agreed with `06-innovation` P2 |
| `verdict` (right, wrong, unconfirmed) and a claim flag | Sentence cards | Gives the real false-rejection rate and self-grade calibration |
| `firstKeyMs` | Typed cards | Time to first keystroke is retrieval onset without thumb speed. Better than the per-character normalisation in F8 |
| `contentRev` | Every session | The site rebuilds on a schedule. Without this, a content change looks like a learning effect |
| `arm` | Per node, at compile | Needed for every comparison below |
| Reading rows: words, seconds, taps, first read or reread | Every read | Agreed with the games report |
| Written text | Writing and personal answers | Agreed with `06-innovation` P4 and the games report |
| Exempt misses, claims and probes from compaction | `compact(…, 365, …)` | Detail older than a year is folded into counts, on a 19-month course |

**B. Fixed instruments.** Everything inside practice measures practice. These four do not.

| Instrument | What it shows | When | Rules that protect it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transfer probe** | A taught structure in a new sentence after a delay | 28 and 90 days, high-value nodes | Quarantined rows: the gate forbids their use anywhere in practice. Typed, cue-neutral, scored on the `focus` span |
| **Remiendo, the C-test** | General proficiency, cheaply | One passage set per band, retaken after at least 3 months | Fixed passages. Pilot on two or three Colombian native speakers, because a passage natives do not ace is broken |
| **Cold read** | Reading rate and comprehension on unseen text | Monthly | Fixed known-word coverage. Not De corrido, which reuses seen text and so measures rereading |
| **Same prompt, every quarter** | Free production | 5 to 8 minutes | Stored text. The tagger computes clauses per sentence, tense range and lint errors per 100 words |

Monthly tracking also needs these on-device health numbers: real seconds per format, minutes per strand, stalled days, claim rate and retest failure rate, and lessons per week before and after Play ships. The last is the games report's own best test, and I agree with it.

**C. Comparisons.** My report proposed holding out a random 10% of nodes. At small numbers that is underpowered. I simulated it with a 70% baseline, 4 delayed attempts per node, and assumed variation between nodes and learners:

| Design | Power for +5 points | +10 | +15 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 learner, 10% holdout | 0.15 | 0.46 | 0.85 |
| 1 learner, paired 50/50 | 0.26 | 0.88 | 0.99 |
| 5 learners, 10% holdout | 0.47 | 0.91 | 0.99 |
| 5 learners, paired 50/50 | 0.93 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 20 learners, 10% holdout | 0.79 | 0.98 | 1.00 |

Rules that follow:

- **Do not hold out fixes.** Honest verdicts and truthful hints ship to everyone. Withholding them to prove them would be absurd.
- **Run paired 50/50 for unproven features only:** the Rayos X panel, Train-it routing, the fingerprint, twin decks against natural rows, El Caso's frame. Pair nodes by band and family, then split each pair by hash.
- **Analyse by assigned arm, not by use.** F1's "after opening against not opening" compares motivated learners with the rest. Compare all nodes in the arm, whether or not anyone opened a panel.
- **Arm by family, not node, where practice spills over.** `x:por-para` and `g:por-para-2` must share an arm.
- **Write the stop rule down first.** The scorecard in section 10 gives one for each feature.

**What these numbers cannot show:** general learning rates, retention, or anything about a population. With a handful of volunteers they catch failures and large effects, and no more. `tools/sim` cannot help, because retention is an input to it. Do not read progress from card accuracy, "learned" counts, streaks or time in app.

## 3. Forced choice and Uno u otro: does binary choice build the decision?

### What I tested

A cue table is a lookup from nearby words to the answer, with no idea of the concept. I built one for each big contrast from half the bank, using the verb and any time word for aspect, the words around *que* for mood, the next word for ser and estar, and the neighbours for por and para. I tested it on the other half at balanced base rates, then on the sentences in the authored scripts. A table with thousands of keys is more than anyone memorises, so read this as a ceiling.

| Contrast | Always the commoner answer, natural sentences | Cue table, held-out bank, balanced | Same table on authored script sentences |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preterite or imperfect | **82%** | 77% | 75% (n=108) |
| Ser or estar | 68% | 85% | **60%** (n=95) |
| Por or para | 55% | 81% | 80% (n=92) |
| Subjunctive or indicative | 58% | 83% | 74% (n=223) |

The authored samples are small, so allow about 9 points either way.

### What that means

1. **Natural sentences cannot supply a decision deck.** A learner who always taps the preterite scores 82%. That is the known preterite default in L2 Spanish (memory: Salaberry), now with a number from this bank. Base rate is a risk in the authored material too: 87 of 108 sentences in the eight past-tense scripts I sampled, which include the imperfect lessons, are imperfect.

2. **The cue means different things for different contrasts.** On mood, nearby words carry 83%, so *quiero que* is a usable rule and matching it is what a fluent writer does. On aspect, no local cue beats simply always saying preterite. The same two-button format is sound practice on the first and rewards a misleading habit on the second.

3. **The English key is worse than it looks.** Only 19% of imperfect sentences in the bank have an English marker (*used to*, *would*, *was -ing*, by regex). The other 81% are plain English pasts. Yet 8 of the 9 read-and-pick items in `x:pret-imp` turn on "used to", and four of its five free-standing your-turn items carry a time word, under a rule that says "Time words help". So rung 0 of the proposed Uno u otro would train a key that fires on one real imperfect in five. Conversely, plain translation from a plain English past is a better aspect test than a binary gap, because the English gives nothing away.

4. **Authored items vary.** The ser and estar scripts are close to cue-neutral at 60%, which is excellent work. The por and para sentences, at 80%, are scarcely harder than natural text. "Dealt from the scripts" is not automatically safe.

5. **Twins exist for only half.** I count 81 twin sets in 46 of 94 scripts. The other 48 contrasts have none.

### How much one right answer proves

A right answer is evidence only if someone who lacks the knowledge would probably miss. Assume a learner who knows gets 95% right (85% when typing).

| Format | Likelihood ratio of a right answer | In units of one twin item |
|---|---|---|
| Two options, natural sentence, against a cue table at 80% | 1.2 | **0.3** |
| Two options, twin set, against a cue table | 1.9 | 1 |
| Three options | 2.9 | 1.6 |
| Typed, against a 5% chance of guessing it | 17 | 4.4 |

So about three natural-sentence taps prove what one twin proves, and four twins prove what one typed answer proves. High speed does not rescue weak items.

### The design rule: choose the format by what decides the contrast

This classification of all 94 is mine, by reading each hook and rule, single-rater. Examples are in the table, and the full list is in the appendix.

| Class | What decides | Count | Examples | Format that builds it | Clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Local trigger** | A word or structure beside the slot | 30 | `x:inf-vs-que-subj`, `x:creo-no-creo`, `x:si-clause-no-future`, `x:muy-mucho`, `x:lo-le` | Binary gap, then typed. Matching the cue is the skill | Yes, after 90% calm accuracy |
| **Intended meaning** | What the speaker means; the frame is silent | 27 | `x:ser-estar-adj`, `x:por-para`, `x:saber-conocer`, `x:relative-ind-subj`, `x:si-real-unreal` | Twins only. Cue with a situation, not a keyword. Then type both sides, as `02` proposes | Only after mastery, twins only |
| **Discourse** | Earlier text, or what the listener knows | 8 | `x:pret-imp`, `x:perfecto-preterito`, `x:aunque-ind-subj`, `x:hay-esta` | Connected passage with gaps (section 6). Translation from plain English pasts | Never |
| **Near-synonym** | Usage and register | 29 | `x:pena-vs-rabia`, `x:antemano-antelacion`, `x:talento-capacidad` | Phrase retrieval in a situation. Not decks | No |

The model item for the second class is already in `02`: one English line, two contexts, *lo conocía* or *lo conocí*.

The fourth class matters for cost. Those 29 are vocabulary and register choices, mostly B2 and C1. Flat proposals of 40 to 80 rows each (mine), or 40 to 60 (`06-innovation`), would spend a large burn where a few phrase-level cards do the job. Near neighbours taught side by side can also interfere with each other (memory: Tinkham 1993, Nation 2000), so the compiler's rule that contrasts come only once both sides are known is right.

**Add a Cue Test beside the Guess Test.** A deck for intended-meaning or discourse contrasts passes only if a cue table scores at most 65% and "always the commoner answer" at most 55%, in CI. Without this, a deck can pass every test in the games report and still reward the wrong thing.

**Ask for the reason after a right answer too.** F3 offers the "why" pick only after a miss. A learner who is right for the wrong reason is exactly the case binary choice hides, and two-tier items exist to catch it (memory: Treagust 1988). On about one right answer in four, ask which reason applies, using the script's own words. It costs about 8 seconds and is a retrieval of the rule.

### Verdicts

| Proposal | Verdict | Learning value per minute | Tier | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forced-choice contrast cards in warm-up | **Keep, by class** | High | Strong for across-session discrimination; Established for forced choice; the class split is my Hypothesis | Twin decks show no gain over natural rows on delayed typed probes, paired by family |
| Uno u otro | **Change.** Local-trigger decks now; meaning decks later; no clock on discourse | High for 30; negative on aspect if timed | Established for trigger drilling; Hypothesis for the game | Deck level does not predict probe accuracy, even on decks that pass the Cue Test |

Two claims need correcting:

- **F3 says "No code change beyond the gate rule."** Not so. A row targeting `x:por-para` reaches `productiveSideCard`, where `typeFormItem` gaps a verb and `answerBelongs` then refuses it, so the review becomes plain translation scored on the whole string, or on 4 of 20 wheel slots an unrelated transform. Translation is a fair decision test, but an unrelated miss then lapses the contrast. It needs `focus` and target scoring.
- **Games P4 says Uno u otro "gives every contrast a spaced return without touching the schedule", and the game section calls it "the only return".** A voluntary game has no schedule and reaches only those who open it. `06-innovation` itself warns that adherence is the binding constraint. The return must be in the warm-up. The game can then add to it.

## 4. Speed games: does time pressure with known items build automaticity?

**What is supported.** Automatisation needs a declarative base and much practice on familiar material (DeKeyser). Nation's fluency strand needs easy material. **What is not.** I know of no direct evidence on typed verb-form sprints, and the games report says the same. It also notes evidence that repetition may matter more than the time squeeze. So the clock is mainly a motivator that may help. It is not the active ingredient.

**The clock fixes blind tapping and rewards rule-of-thumb tapping.** I applied the games report's recommended clock (a miss costs 7.2 seconds, no streak bonus) to two more players it did not model:

| Player on a natural-frequency two-pasts deck | Rights in 60 seconds |
|---|---|
| Careful knower, 90% right, 3.0 s | 14.5 |
| Quick knower, 90% right, 1.8 s | 21.4 |
| Rule of thumb, 80% right, 1.5 s | 16.3 |
| **Always preterite, 82% right, 0.8 s** | **23.5** |
| Blind, 50% right, 0.3 s | 7.7 |

The report's claim that Uno u otro "passes the Guess Test by construction" is true and is not enough. Time pressure pushes learners to the fastest available cue, which is what input-processing work predicts (memory: VanPatten on lexical preference). A balanced deck reduces this but does not remove it: always-preterite drops to blind level, yet a quick cue-table player at 77% and 1.5 seconds still ties the careful knower (14.6 to 14.5). The authored decks the report plans would be better than natural frequency, but nothing in its tests guarantees it.

**Other risks.** Accuracy may not follow volume (Landers replication, in the games report). Rehearsing a shaky form fast can strengthen the wrong one, so a cell or deck should enter speed work only after about 90% calm accuracy over ten attempts. On a phone, total time mostly measures thumbs, which `firstKeyMs` avoids. Transfer is narrow: English meaning to verb form is one route, and it helps free writing only where form access is the bottleneck, probably A2 to B1 and irregular paradigms.

**The honest case for games is dose, not efficiency.** The course gives 242 hours against 700 to 800 guided hours for C1 (English figures, so a rough comparison). Twenty voluntary minutes a week of retrieval is extra time on task, and that is worth having even if each minute teaches less than a warm-up minute. If games take minutes from lessons, the case collapses, as the report says itself.

| Game | Value per minute | Tier | Verdict | Falsified if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relámpago | Medium at A2 to B1, low later | Established in principle; Hypothesis typed | **Keep, gated** at 90% calm accuracy | Armed by tense and person cell, no gain in `firstKeyMs` or accuracy on those forms in translation cards 28 days later |
| De corrido | Medium to high at B1 and up | Established | **Change:** put it in the lesson as the protected reread, and keep the game as extra | Rate rises only on reread text, with no gain on cold reads |
| Rescate | Same as a review, which is high | Strong | **Keep.** Extra spaced retrieval, cheap | Lessons per week fall, or it displaces the warm-up |
| Ordena | Low | Established in apps, thin research | **Change:** later, rule-decided orders only | Not worth testing until the above are done |
| Simón dice | Low to medium | Hypothesis | **Change:** own errors first, then pitfalls, always retype the right form | Error recurrence no better than with a typed review of the same point |
| La escalera | Low to medium | Established for cumulative testing | **Later.** Useful mainly as a diagnostic | A fall at week N does not predict probe failure on that unit |
| El tinto | Low | Hypothesis | **Keep as a habit bet.** The marks allow hill-climbing, so make no learning claim | Lesson days do not rise among players |
| Remiendo | None as learning | Established as an instrument | **Keep, build first** | Passages not aced by natives, or scores do not track the cold read |
| Verbo del día (F13) | Low | Hypothesis | **Drop.** El tinto covers the ritual | |
| Emparejar, Reto semanal, Frase rota, Contrarreloj | Low | Hypothesis | **Drop or merge.** Contrarreloj is Relámpago | |

## 5. Rayos X: learning, or the illusion of it?

Understanding a clear explanation feels like knowing. That is the classic illusion of competence (memory: Koriat and Bjork 2005; Rozenblit and Keil 2002), and my report's objection to "learned" labels applies here too. Five specific problems:

1. **Pull help reaches the wrong people.** Work on help-seeking in tutoring systems finds learners underuse help or misuse it (memory: Aleven et al. 2003). The likely pattern is that engaged learners open the panel on sentences they already got right.
2. **Each opening costs about one retrieval.** Sixty to a hundred words of English takes 20 to 30 seconds, about one translation card at the app's 35-second budget. An opening must be worth more than a retrieval. After a miss, probably. After a right answer, probably not.
3. **The cited evidence is for something else.** Boulton and Cobb's d=0.95 is for learners doing concordance tasks. A list under "More like this" is not a task. Input highlighting raised noticing without clear learning in the sources F1 cites, which it notes are unsettled, and metalinguistic explanation seems to build explicit knowledge more than use (memory: Shintani and Ellis 2013). F1 knows this, which is why it adds Try one, but the panel is then the cost and Try one is the benefit.
4. **"Every sentence can explain itself" is not yet true.** F1's own hand check found 52 of 59 explanations fit, over 25 sentences. If misfits are independent, about **one panel in four** contains a line that does not fit. A false reason at a moment of attention is what we are all criticising in the hints.
5. **F1's trial tests the wrong thing.** It arms Try one, not the panel. The panel is what needs testing.

**In fairness,** a question the learner chose to ask is a moment of real attention, and learner-initiated focus on form tends to produce more uptake (memory: Ellis, Basturkmen and Loewen 2001). It is also the feature adult reviewers will love, and that retention value is real. So it has a place. It is not the third most valuable thing in the app.

**Change.** Split it in three:

- **Pushed at a miss:** one line from the rule or pitfall, plus "See the rule". This belongs to item 2 in the top ten.
- **In the reader:** extend the gloss to form and how it is built (F9's `form+how` on every token), anatomy only, whitelisted. Students of a text ask this question most, and it is cheapest there.
- **The universal panel:** build after items 1 to 8, on a paired trial. Every opening ends in Try one. "More like this" waits until it is a task, such as "What do these share?" with one question.

**Falsified if,** analysed by assigned arm, panel nodes show less than 3 points' gain on delayed production, or fewer than 5% of misses lead to an opening. **Value:** high pushed, low as a panel. **Tier:** Established for feedback at the error, Novel for the panel.

## 6. Reconstruye: rebuilding a chapter from English

The idea is sound. Text reconstruction with comparison is how output forces noticing (memory: Izumi 2002), and nothing else in the app has connected production with a right answer. The problem is the checker.

I took 24 real lines from ch091 and ch125 and wrote the kind of reconstruction a good learner would produce from the English: *Cuando murió* for *Cuando se murió*, *preparó* for *hizo*, *La notaría estaba* for *La notaría quedaba*, the time phrase moved to the end. All 24 are valid Spanish for the English I supplied. The real `matchTranslation` **accepted 0 of 24**. Three got "different order". *Preparó* got "isn't the word here, check the meaning". *Hubiese* got "when does it happen?".

So as specified, nearly every line falls to "Mine says the same thing" and scores as exposure. The only lines scored as productive would be ones reproducing the chapter's exact wording, which rewards memorising the text. That is the failure Bunpro's users describe, and it is the opposite of building a sentence. Other costs:

- **Time.** A four-line beat at the 35-second rate plus comparison is 3 to 5 minutes, 15 to 20% of a session.
- **Data shape.** 53 to 65% of story words are spoken lines, often idiomatic. Narration beats are the ones worth rebuilding.

**Change: build it in stages, and start with what a checker can judge.**

1. **Connected verbs (A2 up, top ten item 8).** The chapter's passage returns with its verbs replaced by infinitives, and the learner types the forms. It suits this purpose well: imperfects are 27 to 41% of past verbs in chapters against 18% in the bank, so base rates are better, and there are about 7 past-tense decisions per 100 words from A2. A 100-word beat takes about two minutes and has one or two valid answers per gap. Mood works the same way at 1 to 6 subjunctives per chapter. Aspect has true alternatives (*Toda la mañana, Vale miró* or *miraba*), so the gate must author an `also` list, and the feedback for those is excellent teaching: what the other form would have said.
2. **Marked lines (B1 up).** One or two lines typed from English, scored on the marked span with `focus`. The rest of the line gets a calm "we'd say".
3. **Full rebuild (B2 up).** Narration beats only, compared side by side, exposure only. No grades.

**Falsified if:** armed by chapter against plain reread, there is no gain on delayed production of the chapter's target nodes. Also test on new sentences: if the gain appears only on lines from that chapter, it is text memorisation, and stage 3 should go. **Value:** stage 1 high, stage 2 medium, stage 3 medium at C1. **Tier:** Established in classrooms, Novel at this scale. F4's dependency on F0.3 stands, since sentence English is also the reader's rescue.

## 7. El Caso

The core items are good. *No quiso firmar* against *No quería firmar* and *Busco un abogado que habla* against *hable* test what a sentence commits the speaker to. Those are intended-meaning and discourse contrasts, tested receptively, and they are the best examples in any of these reports.

The wrapper is where I disagree:

- **Plot leakage.** A reader who remembers the story may know Joaquín refused, without reading the verb. Then the item tests the story. The gate needs a check that the question cannot be answered with the statement removed.
- **Delayed correction.** "Wrong pins get contradicted by a later statement, not by a red cross" removes the immediate reason, which is part of why explicit form-meaning practice works.
- **Time.** Eight statements with reading and a pin take perhaps 4 to 5 minutes. The same eight as twin items take about 2. The frame doubles the cost. Gamification's supported benefit from fiction is behavioural (Sailer and Homner), so the frame must pay in return visits, not in learning.
- **Receptive only.** It builds interpretation, and the literature does not support it as a substitute for production.

**Change.** Move the item type into the contrast decks now: "Did she have a particular lawyer in mind?" Pilot five cases as F5 suggests. Build the rest only if voluntary replay is high.

**Falsified if:** fresh contrast items two weeks later show no gain over the same items without the frame (F5's test, which is right), and completion is no better. **Value:** items high, frame low. **Tier:** Established for the mechanism, Novel for the frame.

## 8. Mis trampas and the error fingerprint

**The feedback half is the strongest proposal in the innovation report**, and `02`'s interference layer is the same idea. Keep both, merged into one rule table. The three batteries disagree on rates (0 of 30 named in `06`, 18 of 50 in mine, 46 of 249 in `02`), because they sampled and scored differently, so nobody should quote a rate. They agree on direction: a large share of hints mislead on exactly the hardest grammar.

**The fingerprint is three things and they should not be rated together.**

| Part | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Logging `given` and `rule` | **Keep, now** | Everything else depends on it |
| Train it, routed by error kind | **Keep** | This is where the learning is, if it follows the rules in `05-product-journeys-upper` H2: choice drills on the actual contrast, not conjugation |
| The screen | **Change: later, and only above a precision bar** | Seeing a list teaches little (memory: error-log evidence such as Lalande 1982 is modest). The risk is real: `weak.ts` already tells a learner "Preterite, missed 15 of 15" when their preterites were right. A named misconception that is false is worse than a generic one. Require about 95% precision on a battery before a rule can be named on screen |
| "Your own mistakes come back" | **Keep as an experiment** | Correcting one's own error is a strong moment, and confident errors seem especially correctable (memory: Butterfield and Metcalfe 2001). Re-exposure to wrong forms can do harm (memory: Jacoby and Hollingshead 1990), though feedback reduces that for recognition lures (memory: Butler and Roediger 2008). So the learner must retype the right version |

Graduated prompts ("locate, then name, then reveal") add a try. That is fine on misses, since the minutes are well spent, but the runner allows one retry today and `gradeOf` would need a rule for the third.

**Falsified if:** hint rules are shipped to all, so test routing instead. Arm by family: recurrence per opportunity over 30 days with Train-it routing against without. **Value:** hints high, routing medium to high, screen low. **Tier:** Strong for prompts, Hypothesis for the rest.

## 9. "Mine says the same thing": how much credit should a self-grade earn?

The reports differ: `05-product-journeys-lower` counts it as correct, `05-product-journeys-upper` grades it Hard, `06-innovation` scores it as exposure, and my report did not say. People judge their own recall too generously even with a model answer to compare, especially on partly wrong answers (memory: Dunlosky and Rawson 2012; Rawson and Dunlosky 2007). L2 self-assessment correlates only moderately with tests (memory: Ross 1998). Judging equivalence in a language you are still learning is harder than either.

So I simulated it, using the app's FSRS settings and a hidden true memory, one productive card for a year, with an assumed 20% false rejection. "Typical" claims 80% of valid answers and 30% of wrong ones. "Gamer" claims everything. Without false rejection a card takes 5.6 reviews at 0.904 recall.

| Credit for a claim | Typical: reviews a year | Recall at review | Days under 80% | Gamer: reviews a year | Recall at review | Days under 80% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| None, grade Again (today) | 11.6 | 0.954 | 0.0% | 11.6 | 0.954 | 0.0% |
| **Good** (`05` lower) | 5.8 | **0.898** | 3.6% | 4.0 | **0.854** | **17.7%** |
| Hard (`05` upper) | 6.9 | 0.917 | 1.3% | 5.3 | 0.878 | 7.5% |
| Partial stability, weight 0.3 | 6.8 | 0.916 | 1.3% | 5.3 | 0.879 | 7.9% |
| Hold, retest in 2 days, second claim is Hard | 7.0 | 0.921 | 2.2% | 5.5 | 0.901 | 10.8% |
| **Hold, retest in 2 days, second claim is a lapse** | 7.4 | 0.926 | 1.9% | 6.1 | 0.912 | 8.8% |

What this shows:

- **Good is unsafe.** It is the only option at or below the 0.90 target for a typical learner (0.894 to 0.900 across false-rejection rates of 5 to 20%), and it is the one gaming pays for.
- **The harm of today's rule is large, and most of it goes with any lesser credit.** Load falls by about 40%.
- **The simulation does not separate the lesser credits.** Hard, partial and hold are about equal on memory, so my choice rests on other grounds.

**Recommendation: no credit, and a retest in a fresh sentence.** The reasons:

- It replaces a self-report with a measurement, because the retest is the only calibration the app can get without AI.
- It gives gaming nothing to win. No number rises.
- Hard raises FSRS difficulty, which barely recovers (the mean-reversion weight is 0.0046), and the fault here was the checker's, not the card's.
- A false rejection on a fresh sentence the second time is unlikely enough that a second claim is fair to treat as a lapse.

**Sensitivity.** The 20% is an assumption. At 5, 10 and 30% false rejection, today's rule gives 6.4, 7.5 and 21.5 reviews a year, and hold gives 5.6, 6.0 and 8.4. The conclusion holds across that range.

**Containing gaming and overclaiming:**

| Control | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Eligibility** | Offer the button only when the checker cannot prove an error: no interference rule fired, and the marked target is present. This removes much of the false-claim supply. Halving the claim rate on wrong answers roughly halves days under 80% |
| **Standard** | Always show the learner's answer against the model with differences marked. Standards improve self-judgement, and this is the noticing moment |
| **No side effects** | A claim never gives a novel pass, a right-first-time tally, a productive unlock, a "learned" count, or anything on a score. It is not a miss in weak spots either |
| **Cap** | About 3 a session below B2, 5 at B2 and up. Past that, the app notes the answer without a button |
| **Calibration** | Track retest failure after claims. If over about 40% across 20 claims, say so kindly: "Lately the ones you marked as fine haven't held up on the next try. Compare carefully." |
| **The loop** | Log sentence and answer locally. In an export they feed the accept burn through the gate, so a valid claim disappears for everyone. This is also the owner's best measure of real false rejection, and he should hand-audit a sample |

Copy also matters. It should invite comparison ("Same meaning?"), not assert equality, since the button is mostly a noticing prompt.

**Falsified if:** retest pass rate after claims is no better than after plain misses, which would mean claims carry no information. Then remove the button and rely on acceptance. **Value:** high, as it removes a harm. **Tier:** Established for comparing with a model; credit policy is simulated only.

## 10. How much reading, and what does it cost?

**The 500,000 target was mine and it does not fit.** I wrote "15 minutes a day at 150 words a minute". That adds 15 minutes to a fixed-length lesson, at a speed few learners reach before B2.

**What exists.** With assumed speeds of 50, 80, 110, 140 and 170 words a minute by band, first-pass reading is 1.0 to 2.1 minutes a session, or **12.6 of 242 hours, about 5%**. Report 04's 11 to 24% of plan minutes includes rereads and questions.

**What extra volume costs**, spread by what each band can absorb:

| New words | Hours (speed ±25%) | A1 | A2 | B1 | B2 | C1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300,000 | 41 (33 to 54) | 1.7 min, 9% | 2.8, 13% | 4.3, 17% | 5.8, 21% | 7.2, 24% |
| 400,000 | 54 (43 to 72) | 2.3, 12% | 3.8, 17% | 5.8, 23% | 7.7, 28% | 9.7, 32% |
| 500,000 | 68 (54 to 90) | 2.9, 14% | 4.7, 22% | 7.2, 29% | 9.7, 35% | 12.1, 40% |

These sit on top of today's reading blocks. At 500,000 a B1 session would be about 44% reading while B1 still teaches its grammar. That is not defensible.

**What is really needed.** Nation's 171,000 and 300,000 are what reading alone needs for 12 meetings per word. Fluidez has spaced review, so reading has a different job: meeting taught words in varied contexts, and being the only contact with receptive words. So I derive demand from the words:

- 1,244 taught words at B1 and up, at 8 meetings in connected text.
- 1,830 receptive words at 4 meetings. Report 04 found 1,265 of them get zero encounters for an average learner.
- That is about 17,300 meetings. At a planned 6 to 8% density of target words, it takes **about 220,000 to 290,000 new words**.

Density is an assumption, and it only holds if the writing is planned for recycling. Unplanned text would need several times more.

**Recommendation.** Fund 250,000 to 300,000 new words in the lesson path, about 350,000 to 400,000 with the story. That agrees with report 04's plan of about 400,000 and replaces my number. Build beyond that if wanted as a free library: it costs build time, not lesson time, and a learner who wants more gets more. The owner's "strongest design" points to building it. It does not justify spending lesson minutes on it.

**Where the minutes come from:** review load after honest verdicts (top ten item 1), first-contact tests and cognates (item 5), and the reading-led B2 and C1 day from `05-product-journeys-upper`, which suits "few and long". Language-focused work is 69 to 84% of time, so the swap has room. Report 04 adds urgency: an average standard-tier learner meets only 19 of 380 planned B2 words and 11 of 332 C1 words, which is a time problem before it is a reading problem.

**What this will not do.** Per-word pick-up from reading is low (memory: Waring and Takaki 2003), and meta-analytic gains are moderate (Nakanishi 2015). Reading here is consolidation and fluency, not a vocabulary engine.

**Falsified if:** taught words met 6 or more times in the library show no retention gain over words that were not, given equal reviews, or cold-read rate does not rise. **Value:** medium per minute, large in total. **Tier:** Strong for direction, with modest per-word gains.

## 11. Evidence weighting by format, and a grammar mastery estimate

**Evidence weighting: drop as proposed.** I said a recognition pass should grade Hard at most. Three problems, all mine:

1. **Wrong card.** The productive wheel is 8 translate, 8 cloze, 4 transform. Productive cards are never graded by multiple choice, and `isLearned` reads the productive card, so recognition passes cannot inflate "learned". The issue is confined to receptive cards, which step aside once production takes over.
2. **Wrong tool.** Simulated on a perfect receptive card with half its reviews multiple choice, Hard takes reviews a year from 4.0 to **6.5**, and difficulty from 5.24 to **7.12**, which barely recovers. FSRS reads Hard as "this was hard".
3. **Wrong order.** If the distractors are fixed (top ten item 7), most of the reason disappears.

If weighting is ever justified, the app already has the right tool: `PARTIAL_WEIGHT` interpolation moves stability without touching difficulty (5.1 reviews, difficulty 5.26). But first log `fmt`, then ask whether the next review's pass rate differs by the format of the last pass. **Falsified if** it does not differ. Then there is nothing to weight. **Value:** low. **Tier:** Hypothesis.

Read-only cards recording Good is a plain bug. It should go regardless.

**Mastery estimate: keep it silent.** It matters only if it changes a decision, such as re-drilling or the "learned" label, and with no learners there is nothing to calibrate it on. Do not use BKT or anything fitted. Compute the simple rule from my P7 in shadow, from the log: recent attempts across at least K verbs or frames and two formats. Show nothing yet. **Falsified if,** per node within learner, it does not predict transfer-probe success better than FSRS stability alone (AUC gain under about 0.03). With hundreds of nodes one learner can answer that. Probes come first. **Value:** low until it drives decisions. **Tier:** Hypothesis.

## 15. Method and limits

**Scripts and output** are in `/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-Matthew-Fluidez/40ef6734-a9c2-4284-b528-c9cc038164e6/scratchpad/xreview-sla/`: `cue.ts`, `cue2.ts`, `cue3.ts`, `cue4.ts` (cue players), `recon.ts` (reconstruction and chapter tense mix), `selfgrade.ts`, `selfgrade2.ts`, `selfgrade3.ts` (FSRS policies), `power.py` (holdouts). All used real content and real app code. Nothing in `app/` changed. I did not drive the browser for this review.

**Limits that matter:**

- **No real learners.** Every effect is inferred. Every simulation takes true memory from FSRS itself, and the self-grade profiles and false-rejection rates are assumptions.
- **Cue tables are ceilings.** Regex extraction of verbs and English markers is approximate, sentences with more than one past verb were excluded, and authored samples are small.
- **The 24 reconstructions are mine,** not a sample of learner answers. I chose them to be natural, so read 0 of 24 as a statement about capacity, not a rate.
- **The contrast classification is single-rater** and several contrasts sit on a boundary.
- **Reading speeds are assumed.** Target-word density is assumed. Minute shares are card time only and exclude reading, scenes and writing.
- **Power figures** assume independent nodes and my chosen variances.
- **Time estimates** for El Caso, Rayos X openings and Reconstruye are arithmetic, not observation.
