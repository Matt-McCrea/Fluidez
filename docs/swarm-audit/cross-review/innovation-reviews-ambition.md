# Cross-review: the Innovation Specialist tests the ambition

Role: Innovation Specialist, challenging whether the product ambition is broad enough. Reviewed 1 November 2026 on branch `rebuild`. I read all fifteen reports (every verdict, recommendation and priority section; the six linguistics reports at verdict and recommendation level only). I then ran my own measurements, listed in section 13. Nothing in `app/` was changed. Names such as Carreras and Expediente are working titles, not screen copy.

**Verdict.** If every critical fix lands, Fluidez is the most honest Spanish grammar course I know of, very good to about B1 and competent on paper above it. That is not what the owner asked for. Every critical finding in the fifteen reports is a repair to trust, and trust is a reason not to leave. It is not a reason to choose. After all of them, "C1" prose still runs at 8.4 words a sentence, the average finisher still sits at about 83% of a Quiroga story and about 74% of today's Colombian news leads, and the plot still describes a library the learner never gets to open.

The specialists found the right assets. They think too small in three places: what B2 and C1 are, where reading comes from, and what the learner is working towards.

| Big call | Ruling |
|---|---|
| 300,000 to 500,000 words on a separate shelf beside the story | **Change.** Wrong shape. The plot has already named the library. Write that |
| B2 and C1 as units of days with a weekly long read added | **Change.** Invert it. The document is the lesson, and grammar attaches to it |
| A course that ends "You've finished. Reviews keep it fresh" | **Change.** It ends in a real book the learner watched being written, then their own Second Part |
| Rayos X, Mis trampas, contrast sets, evidence can-dos | **Keep.** This is the core, with changes below |
| Paste any Spanish (ranked 14th of 16) | **Raise.** It is the only honest yardstick and the only exit |
| Nine games | **Change.** Two games, one reading run, one yardstick, later a daily |
| El Taller, anchored levels, hash arms, challenge links, Ordena, La escalera, Verbo del día | **Drop** or defer. Section 8 |
| Dozens of separate builds across the reports | **Change.** Eight builds. Section 9 |

## 1. If every critical fix lands, what is this product?

Measured against the combination the owner asked for:

| The owner asked for | After every critical fix | Honest state |
|---|---|---|
| Depth of an excellent grammar course | Contrasts run, one model of the subjunctive, rules no longer overclaim | **Close to B2.** C1 scripts are still "B1 topics with a C1 title" (L-C1) |
| Vocabulary of a sophisticated system | About 2,500 words delivered if V1 lands. Frames, families and collocations are separate later layers | **No.** A solid list, not a word-knowledge system, and far short of reading level |
| Reading of a graded reader | The reader works and placed learners get the right chapter. There are still 87,000 words of it | **No.** A very good reader with almost nothing to read |
| Evidence-based learning | Design is strong and well argued | **Informed, not proven.** No human has been measured. Every report says so, and none proposes fixing it (section 6) |
| Engagement people return to | The story is real. Streak is sound. Above A2 there is little else | **No from B1 on.** Upper: the app "keeps a B1+ learner neither accurate nor interested" |

Here is the gap in one table. I ran the real tagger over real Spanish and asked what share of running words a learner owns. "Taught through B1" is about 1,800 words, which is what the Vocabulary audit finds the average finisher actually receives (1,822 of 2,528).

| Text | Words | Words per sentence | Taught through B1 | Taught through C1 | Whole graph, 4,554 lexemes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quiroga, *Cuentos de amor de locura y de muerte*, 1917 | 16,702 | 15.6 | 83.1% | 85.1% | 89.3% |
| Spanish Wikipedia, La Candelaria | 1,387 | 38.5 | 82.4% | 86.5% | 92.0% |
| El Tiempo, Colombia section, headlines and leads, today | 417 | 17.4 | 74.2% | 78.1% | 89.2% |
| **Fluidez C1 chapters** | 30,299 | **8.4** | 90.4% | 94.8% | 97.5% |

Lemmatisation fails on some inflected words outside the lexicon (*hijos*, *fundó*), so real text is understated by a point or two. The news sample is small. The shape is not in doubt: the course's own C1 reads easier than any real Spanish I tried, and one running word in four or five stays unknown in real text. Nothing in the critical list moves that much. Reading will not be repaired into existence.

So the honest one-line description of the repaired product is: *a careful Spanish course with a good story that does not lie to you.* That beats Babbel and Kwiziq and it is still not a product anyone seeks out.

## 2. The one sentence

Innovation's line is: "every sentence can explain itself, and the app knows your mistakes by name." It is the best line in the fifteen reports, and it describes a tool. People choose an outcome, and they forgive a tool for being hard if they believe in where it goes.

Mine:

> **The Spanish course that ends with you reading a real book, can explain every sentence on the way there, and never calls anything yours until you have used it unaided.**

Three promises, and each is a checkable claim: a destination, an account of every sentence, and honest credit. A rival with an LLM can offer the second loosely. Nothing in Innovation's market survey offers all three, and the third is why the first can be believed.

The sentence fails today on all three counts, which is the point of the sequence in section 11.

## 3. Where the specialists think too small: reading, and what B2 and C1 are

### 3.1 Is 300,000 words built on a soap opera the right idea?

No, but not for the reason the reports imply. There are three numbers in play: 300,000 (Curriculum, La repisa), 400,000 (Vocabulary R2, plus La Gaceta) and 500,000 (Acquisition P9). All three treat volume as the target and propose a new shelf beside the story, with Curriculum, Acquisition and Vocabulary each saying the story must stay as it is. Three objections:

1. **Volume is the wrong target.** A gate that counts words will get words. What the research supports is meetings: 92% of C1 words appear in fewer than 3 later chapters (Acquisition 2.8). A million words that do not recycle fail too. Recycling, sentence length and genre should be gated. The word total is an output.
2. **A generic model-written shelf is a commodity, and the weakest possible use of this app.** LingQ, Readlang and every graded-reader series compete there. Bland passages on recycling and the history of coffee are also where drafted text is most likely to go dead, and the owner's rule that natural Spanish beats checker legality gets harder to hold.
3. **The binary is false.** The soap opera's *form* is wrong for B2 and C1: "Name: line" at every band, 53 to 65% of words spoken. Its *world* is the best asset in the product. Keep the world and change the form.

The evidence for this was in the content and nobody put it together. **The plot has already named its library.** I scanned all 219 chapters. Regex counts are rough, and "cuaderno" also catches Lucía's sketchbook:

| Document the plot depends on | Mentions | In chapters | What the learner actually gets to read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hernando's book and his 32 notebooks | 98 | 56 | Fragments, and the 91-word last page in ch202 |
| Rosa's 47 unsent letters | 105 | 53 | Excerpts of 16 to 39 words, from a handful of letters |
| Mariana's articles, the newspaper | 70 | 35 | A headline and a lead, about 40 words at u45 |
| Actas, contracts, complaints, the deed | 78 | 47 | Short quotes |
| Clemencia's report, Altamira's statements | 42 | 24 | 61 words at most |
| The podcast | 25 | 8 | Nothing as a transcript |
| Tomás's prize essay | 22 | 8 | Three or four single lines |
| Leonor's reviews | 15 | 10 | 73 and 58 words |
| Vale's and Rosa's recipes | 24 | 17 | Nothing as a recipe |

Everything inside quotation marks across the whole serial comes to 6,194 words in 524 pieces. The longest is 123 words. So the story keeps telling the learner about texts that matter to everyone in it, and then does not show them. ch144 is the clearest case: "En español tiene tres frases. En inglés tiene once." A plot turn hangs on comparing a Spanish document with its English version, in a course whose owner wants more translation everywhere, and the learner sees neither. Vocabulary R4 comes closest, and then proposes keeping these "separate from the story so that it stands up as real genre". I think that is backwards. These documents matter because the learner cares what is in them.

### 3.2 The reading architecture I would build

| Layer | Bands | What it is | New words | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The serial** | A1 to C1 | The 219 chapters, unchanged | 0 | Spine, emotion, hooks |
| **Carreras** | A1 to B2 | Hernando's 32 notebooks: one fare, one standalone story, one a day | 150,000 to 200,000 | The daily reading life and the volume |
| **Expedientes** | B1 to C1 | The documents themselves, in real genres, as the core of upper lessons | About 150,000 | Prose shape, genre, can-do evidence, translation |
| **El libro** | C1 | Hernando's memoir, as an actual book in real prose | 60,000 to 70,000 | The destination |
| **Afuera** | B2 up | Public-domain and openly licensed real text, plus paste your own | Unbounded | The exit, and the yardstick |
| **Segunda parte** | C1 and after | The learner's own writing | Theirs | What you own at the end |

**Carreras** deserves the most attention, because it solves six problems at once. ch175 already says what is in the notebooks: «14 de marzo de 1983. Calle 26. Señora con gallina. La gallina no pagó». A priest who drinks half a bottle of aguardiente between the Jiménez and the Setenta y Dos. A fare is:

- **Standalone.** So a placed learner has something at their level from day one, with no need to know who Yesid is (R3, H6, C4 all soften at once).
- **Graded by the notebook's year.** The early notebooks can be terse, as the 1983 entry above is, and the later ones can run to pages. A log is also the one genre where A1 Spanish is natural, so telegraphic lines stop being a distortion. The owner's objection to "Hello. Sorry. Closed." was to dialogue pretending to be speech.
- **Varied inside one world.** Every passenger brings a trade, a neighbourhood, a register and a subject: a doctor, a lawyer, a footballer, a widow. That is the honest home for *medio ambiente*, *derechos humanos*, *cambio climático*, which have zero occurrences today (V8).
- **Past-tense narrative prose.** The thing the course has least of.
- **Five minutes.** The Five-minute learner today gets "the same seven items, and the bar never moves". A fare is a better five minutes, and a better low-energy door than a puzzle.
- **Supply at the right scale.** Five minutes a day at about 100 words a minute over 590 days is at best roughly 290,000 words, less at lower bands where reading is slower. That is most of R2's volume from one daily habit, without touching lesson time (section 4).

**Why not start with real text?** Because of section 1. At 78 to 86%, real text is unusable until late, and it is why the bridge has to exist. Running stories reduce vocabulary load (Hwang and Nation 1989; narrow reading, Schmitt and Carter 2000, both from memory). A reader who knows the case already knows half the vocabulary of the next document about it.

**El libro** is the part most at risk of being vanity, so it needs a kill rule (section 3.5). Curriculum's decision 6 asks for a payoff after a slow burn of twenty months, and gets a hint at u60. This is the payoff. The plot literally produces the object: Hernando finishes the book the night before he dies, and ch202 reads its last page at his funeral. The learner has watched about thirty chapters of it being fought over, has read the raw notebooks, and can now read what he made of them. "Señora con gallina" at A2 becomes a two-page set piece at C1, and the learner has met both versions. The first page, without spoilers, sits on Home from week 1, with each word lit by whether the learner owns it. Games' "last chapter" device is the seed of this. I would make it a real page rather than a ratio, because a learner can test it by trying to read it.

**Segunda parte.** ch204 ends: Tomás opens a new notebook and writes one line, «Segunda parte». The app currently ends with "You've finished the course. Reviews keep it all fresh." Every piece of writing the learner has saved belongs there, and the post-course programme Upper asks for already has its name.

### 3.3 B2 and C1 stop being units of days

Upper says upper days should be reading-led. Curriculum 6.3 gets closest: one topic, 2 or 3 passages, a grouped frame block, mixed practice and one piece of writing. But it still starts from "all frames for the function", the passages are unconnected to the plot, and 6.4 then adds "a parallel weekly long read" on top. Innovation adds El Caso on checkpoint days and Reconstruye on output days. Vocabulary adds a Gaceta with 2 to 4 pieces per unit. Each of these is right, and together they are a bolted-on pile. They are one lesson shape.

**The Expediente.** About 150 lessons of 45 to 55 minutes replace 190 days of 28 to 30, which honours "few and long" for the first time. Today's rendered u58 day delivers its chapter after 40 cards, including typing "He's a diamond in the rough." twice. Here is unit 45 as one lesson, using what the plot already provides:

| Step | What happens | Absorbs |
|---|---|---|
| Gap | The compiler diffs the documents against this learner and serves exactly what they lack: "9 words and one structure short of reading this cold". No two learners get the same pre-work | V1 at upper bands, section 5.1 |
| Read | Mariana's front page, about 650 words of real newspaper prose. ch142's headline is already written: «El exfuncionario Rengifo habría recibido 400 millones...». Then the gossip post, already written: «La pastelera del café estaría embarazada». Then Altamira's statement, Spanish and English | R4, R6, R2 |
| Evidence | Eight questions only the form answers. Did the paper say he took the money? Who is claiming this? ch142 already teaches this: «"Habría" significa que no lo digo yo.» | F5, R5. Longest-option cue goes |
| Duel | Ten contrasts drawn from the shared store on this document's point | F3 |
| Translate | The investor statement, line by line, English to Spanish, checked against aligned variants, then seen beside the original | F4, "more translation", mediation |
| Write | Mariana's correction note, 120 to 150 words: lint, target features, model comparison, one rewrite. Kept | H5, F12 |
| Chapter | ch142 as the reward: the café reacts, the lights go out | The serial stays |

The grammar node is found in the document instead of the document being written to carry the grammar node, which is the cause of R12's stuffing.

**The existing 95 B2 and C1 chapters stay exactly as they are.** Here I side with Curriculum against Vocabulary R6 and R11. R6's band specification is right, and it belongs on the new documents. Rewriting chapters to raise their sentence length would damage the best writing in the product to fix a problem the documents solve. The funeral in ch202 works because it is short.

**The rhythm changes, and nobody said so.** Daily lessons of 50 minutes will not survive 19 months. At B2 and above, three or four long lessons a week and a short fare on the other days. That needs three things no report joins up:

- Multi-day resume. `isResumable` requires the same learner day and 3 hours (`store/ui.ts:162`, `RESUME_WINDOW_MS`), so a 50-minute lesson dies at midnight. Lower's "slices" and Upper's M7 are the same requirement.
- A fare or a reading run counts as a legitimate day for the streak (Acquisition P9.3).
- Learners still do not choose length. The lesson is fixed; the learner chooses when to stop for the day.

### 3.4 What this removes

La Gaceta, La repisa, the Acquisition library, the Lower reading shelf, El Caso as a separate block and 40 to 60 separate case files, Reconstruye as a separate daily block, the Vale correspondence as a simulated relationship, band-spec rewrites of chapters, and most of "questions in the warm-up". That is the test of whether this is a choice or a blend: seven proposals stop being separate work.

### 3.5 The case against my own proposal

- **One world, one authorial voice.** R4's complaint survives partly. Passengers, documents and letters vary the voice, and Afuera must start at B2 for that reason. One third of C1 reading should be real text.
- **Fake authenticity.** A model-written newspaper is not a newspaper. The app should say so: this is a made-up paper in a made-up case, and here is a real one. Real text must arrive before the learner feels sheltered.
- **Only the few reach it.** Holt's 5% problem: if the distinctive core lives at C1, almost nobody sees it. That is why the first page, Why?, the traps and the fares all arrive in week one.
- **The prose might be dull.** Then it should not ship. Kill rule: a Colombian editor reads the first 30 fares and the first three chapters of the book. If they would not read on, the book is dropped and the C1 destination becomes a glossed public-domain work.
- **Public domain is old.** Quiroga has *fué* and *vió*, and morphine in the plot. R4 and Upper name only literary authors. Add what nobody named: Spanish Wikipedia (CC BY-SA, modern, encyclopedic) and official Colombian text such as the 1991 Constitution and court rulings, which is real institutional Spanish for `d:u55-institutional-spanish`. Rights need checking per text, including Mistral, whom R4 lists.

## 4. The arithmetic nobody did: the minutes

Fifteen specialists each added to a 20 to 30 minute slot. Innovation's section 6.7 is the only place that asks where time comes from, and it stops at two sources. My rough sum for a B1 session, using the reports' own figures:

| Item | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Today's B1 budget | 25 |
| Honest reviews: the 8-minute warm-up assumes 10 s each, about 48 reviews. At 18 to 25 s (Acquisition 2.11) | +6 to +12 |
| Reserved word time, V1, against what is delivered today | +2 |
| Reading raised to a third of the session, R2 | +4.5 |
| A contrast block on each day it runs, F3 | +3 |
| A Reconstruye beat on days it runs, F4 | +3 |
| **Demand** | **About 45 to 50** |

So if everything lands inside the lesson, a B1 session is about twice its slot, and Acquisition has already shown that an honest clock turns 76 to 90% of placed sessions into review-only days. **No new block should be approved until it names what it replaces.** The exits, in order:

1. **Reading leaves the lesson up to B1.** A fare of 3 to 5 minutes, its own daily slot, counted. By my estimate this raises meaning-focused input from Acquisition's 8 to 13% to about a quarter of total time, which is Nation's guideline.
2. **Reviews get cheaper.** Format mix under load (P8.3), cognates receptive-only (H6: 44% of C1 lexemes are near-identical to their gloss), and section 5.5.
3. **The lesson stops spending minutes on weak recognition.** First contact at B1 and above is one typed retrieval (H6). In u34, 11 of the first 16 cards are multiple choice on new words with unrelated distractors.

Per-format timing (Acquisition's item 10) therefore comes before any new block, not after. I would put a CI check on it: no band's planned session may exceed its budget at honest timing.

## 5. Uses of the graph, engine, tagger and bank that nobody proposed

### 5.1 `requires` run backwards

Everywhere in the app, `requires` answers "is this learner allowed to see this text?" Run the other way, it answers **"what would it take for this learner to read this?"** Text minus known gives a set of nodes, and the compiler can serve that set. That is the personal pre-work in the Expediente. It is also a real answer to the old app's themed weeks: "I have a contract to read on Friday." Paste it, and get a session. Nobody proposed this, and I think it is the strongest unused capability in the codebase, because `compileSession` is pure and a plan is plain JSON.

### 5.2 Vocabulary pulled by the reading ahead

V1 says reverse the priority, and that is right. But the owed queue is then served in plan order, which V4 shows is corrupted. I measured a realistic learner (known = taught through a band), taking 200 words from the owed queue, in plan order against order of frequency in the next 30 chapters:

| Learner, position | Chapters ahead, now | Plan order | Reading pull | Wikipedia, plan | Wikipedia, pull |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Through A2, at u28 | 89.7% | 93.7% | **96.2%** | 80.5% | 80.0% |
| Through B1, at u42 | 91.6% | 95.1% | **95.9%** | 85.3% | 85.2% |
| Through B1, at u52 | 90.7% | 91.4% | **94.5%** | 85.3% | 82.6% |

Pull wins clearly on the text the learner is about to read, and costs little on general text until C1, where it does cost, on a small Wikipedia sample: *cooperativa* and *estatuto* are plot words. So the rule is pull within the owed queue at A2 and B1, and at B2 and above weight by real-corpus frequency as well. This also makes R7 a design rather than a bug fix. Note what neither order does: 200 words barely move Quiroga (83.1 to 83.8%). Only reading volume plus tap-to-card moves that.

### 5.3 The tagger on the learner's own writing: avoidance

F2 and the contrastive layer catch what learners get wrong. Nobody catches what they never do. A learner can pass every drill on the imperfect subjunctive and never once choose it, which is the classic finding about avoidance (Schachter 1974; Laufer and Eliasson 1993, from memory). Once writing is stored (F0.2), the tagger can report, with illustrative figures: "You have written 14,000 words here. You have used *porque* 212 times and *ya que* twice. You have been taught *aunque* with the subjunctive and have never chosen it." That is a fingerprint of omission, it is deterministic, and it feeds the writing constraint on the next task. It obeys the standing rule that free production is signal only. Limits: 37 grammar nodes have no tagger key (Curriculum), absence is not proof, and a minimum word count must apply. No tutor can do this at scale and no LLM product does it honestly over two years of a person's text.

### 5.4 The gate on the app's own interface

**The app turns into Spanish as you learn it.** Every UI string gets a Spanish twin, tagged by the same tagger. A string flips when its `requires` are known and retrievable. Check becomes Revisar in week 2. Next becomes Siguiente. "Not yet" becomes "Todavía no". By B2 the feedback is Spanish, and by C1 the explanations are. Tap for English, always. Destructive actions never flip.

R10 proposes fading hooks and notes, and Games has taught chunks as reactions. Nobody proposed the whole shell, gated per learner. There are about 670 distinct strings by a rough scan. It is cheap, gate-checkable, the most frequent comprehensible input in the app, visible by the second week, and impossible to explain to a friend without showing them. It also dramatises the third promise: the app addresses you in exactly the Spanish it knows you own. If only one idea in this review ships this month, this should be it.

### 5.5 Reading pays reviews

Vocabulary R9 and Innovation F9 make a tap a soft miss. Take the other half too. A receptive card whose word is met and not tapped, in running text, gets bounded partial credit. A tap is a miss. This is not Games' forbidden hit-only credit, because misses count, and `creditSentence` already bounds it. The target skill of a receptive card is exactly recognising the word in context. Then reading *replaces* flashcard minutes at upper bands, which is the only way the receptive tier scales: nobody reviews 5,000 receptive cards. Status: hypothesis, and Games' simulation is a reason to test it before trusting it.

### 5.6 The engine: transfer to a verb you have never met

Acquisition P13 wants transfer probes, and P3 shows the app cannot tell memory of a sentence from a rule. The engine can ask for a form of a real regular verb this learner has never met, glossed on the card. It cannot have been memorised. It is the wug test (Berko 1958, from memory) with real Spanish, it needs no authored content, and it suits placement, where 91% of today's upper items test tense or verb form on known material. A cheap, strong addition.

### 5.7 The bank as a translation memory

72,693 aligned pairs is a graded Linguee. Look up searches grammar and words, and "How do I say...?" is the most common thing an adult wants. Phrase probes against the English side, exact substrings: *it depends* 47, *on the other hand* 46, *I can't stand* 40, *as far as I know* 19, *I'm fed up* 26, but *it's up to you* 3, *landlord* 1, *hang out* 0, *sorry to hear* 0. So it is useful and patchy. Filter to what the learner can read, and fall back to "not here yet". The bank's Spanish and English text is 2.1 MB gzipped and already split by unit, so this needs a small English index, lazily loaded.

### 5.8 Settle the runtime tagger argument

Innovation says ship it lazily. Architecture says do not ship it, and write `content/explain/` at about 2 MB gzip. Vocabulary says precompute per token. All three miss what decides it. **Learner text cannot be precomputed.** Lint, avoidance, Why on your own sentence and paste all need the tagger on the device. Measured: data files 82 KB gzipped, code 64 KB gzipped before minifying. Innovation measured 1.1 to 1.5 ms per sentence in Node; my run on all four texts, without chunk matching, stayed under 0.4 ms. No phone was tested.

Resolution: ship the tagger as a lazy chunk, and store only a small gate-checked **override table** for authored sentences where it is wrong. Architecture's precision concern stands: the P10 fixes come first, and a whitelist of feature families governs what may be asserted. Honest limit from my run: 43 to 56% of real-text sentences come back low confidence against 7% on C1 chapters, and unknown part of speech is 3.4 to 5.0% against 0.9%. So on pasted text the panel degrades to anatomy only, and needs F14's 30,000-lemma gloss list.

## 6. What is still missing that no specialist named

1. **A human.** Every report lists "no real learners" as a limitation, and then proposes telemetry or hash arms. Nobody proposes the obvious step: eight to twelve English-speaking adults, four weeks, watched. This should happen before the largest burn. As far as the reports and project notes show, the only human who has played this two-year course is its owner, for a few units.
2. **A Colombian editor.** The owner's standing feedback says every content type gets a native-reader review. I found no sign that a human native reader has reviewed any of it, several reports flag items that need one, and 13 claims came back from verification marked `native-check`. The same reports then recommend 300,000 to 500,000 more words of drafted text. About 400,000 words at a light edit of 1,500 words an hour is roughly 270 hours (my estimate). Nobody costed it. Cost is secondary, and this one is not optional.
3. **Device roles.** 55 to 61% of cards are typed, and no specialist tried this on a real phone. The natural home of a long Expediente is a keyboard, and the natural home of a fare is a phone. Progress lives in one browser, and export and import is backup, not sync. Architecture notes this as a limit in one line. "No server" is not on the owner's list of standing decisions: `redesign.md` says offline. A small encrypted sync blob does not break offline. Owner's call, and I would ask.
4. **Pragmatics was never audited.** Zero mentions in fifteen reports. The content has real material (`p:request-ladder`, `p:refuse-es-que`, `g:softening`, u42), and "polite" appears in 113 of 916 scripts, so this is an audit gap, not a known hole. But nothing checks it, nothing practises it as a decision, and in Bogotá a correct, blunt sentence does more social damage than a wrong tense. It should be a trap family: "Right Spanish. Too blunt for a stranger." The sociocultural inventory of the PCIC has not been reviewed by anyone.
5. **A definition of who this is for.** Sharing grids, challenge links and leagues assume an audience, and there is a GitHub Pages URL. If this is Matthew's app for a few people, a third of the Games report is moot. If it is a public product, distribution is the biggest missing feature. Nobody asked.

## 7. What would be embarrassing to ship

In front of one competent Spanish teacher, in one sitting:

| What they would see | Source |
|---|---|
| Voseo and future subjunctive typed as "the right version", and *Poco no me sirvió, la verdad* marked correct, in the lesson that warns against it | L-C1, Lower C2 |
| A tap on *la* showing a note about Rosa's door, for a third of all tokens | R1 |
| *la parte*: "bulletin, report". *desgracia*: "disgrace". *solo* never "only" | V5 |
| 41 of 47 valid alternatives rejected at advanced level, with false reasons | Upper C3 |
| "It comes up in week 165" in a 60-week course | Upper H1 |
| 212 words of "the cat sat on the mat" getting three of four ticks at C1, then 60 can-do ticks including "I can write a sustained argumentative essay" | Upper H5, H7 |

| "C1" reading at 8.4 words a sentence | Section 1 |
| Week 1 story, "Hola, Bogotá", served to a learner placed at week 60 | R3 |

And these, which the current plans would create:

- **Half a million words nobody Colombian has read**, in a product whose claim is that it is truthful about Spanish.
- **The words "evidence-based" on a product no learner has used.** Say "built on the research" until the claim can be shown.
- **A C1 label.** Curriculum is right: stage names, honest onboarding, and say plainly what is not covered. 242 hours is not C1.
- **A relationship faked by branching.** A Vale who replies warmly to what she cannot read will be noticed.
- **A Wordle that rejects correct Spanish every morning**, to every user at once.

## 8. Vanity projects

Ambitious ideas whose appeal exceeds what they do for a learner. This is a judgement, and a harsh one on purpose.

| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| **El Taller** (F11) | The best demo and the weakest evidence. It rests on `reinflect`, the machinery behind Lower C2 and the C1 voseo failures. ## 10. The core, and what to say no to

**The distinctive core is three things, and only three.**

1. **The sentence accounts for itself.** Why?, named traps, avoidance, and the shell that speaks only what you own. The engine and tagger facing the learner.
2. **The choice is the practice.** One contrast store, everywhere, because the choices are what defeat English speakers.
3. **A world worth reading your way through.** Fares daily, documents as lessons, a real book at the end, real Spanish beyond it, and your own Second Part.

Honest credit runs through all three: nothing is yours until you have used it unaided.

**Say no to:**

- A shelf of generic graded passages.
- Rewriting the chapters.
- Any new lesson block that does not name what it replaces.
- A second daily ritual.
- More than three public measures of progress.
- Points by another name, including "levels" built on assumed difficulty.
- CEFR labels as claims.
- Showing any number the Five Tests have not passed.
- Say it aloud.
- Shipping drafted prose no Colombian has read.
- Social features before there is anyone to be social with.

## 11. The sequence that makes it visibly different soonest

Order is by when a learner would notice. Sizes assume subagents with review.

**Stage 0. The floor, one to two weeks.** Nothing to celebrate, and all of it would be caught in a first session. Everything in section 7's first table. Free pass, mood and aspect hints, placement ladder and crash, transform whitelist, R1, chapter window, warm-up cap, honest scene verdicts and can-dos, stage labels. Capture: `given`, `err`, `first`, writing text, reads with taps, monthly snapshot. Export and import. Architecture P1 to P3. Claims applied.

**Stage 1. It already feels different, two to three weeks.** All of this is visible in the first fortnight of a learner's life.

- Named traps on the first miss.
- Why? in the reader and after every card.
- Open reader: next chapter, per-sentence English, tap to card, known share.
- Puentes on day 1.
- The shell starts turning: forty commonest strings.
- The first page of the book on Home, lit by what you own.
- Thirty fares in a daily slot.

Then **put it in front of eight to twelve people.** If the fares and the traps do not land, learn it here.

**Stage 2. One burn, five faces, four to six weeks.** The contrast store and `focus` together. Contrast scripts taught. Reviews that contrast. Train it that trains. Uno u otro. Placement and map rebuilt on it. Target scoring and three verdicts. Then per-format timing and the CI budget check, because Acquisition is right that the checker comes first.

**Stage 3. One Expediente, two to three weeks. This is the decisive step.** Unit 45, "Habría", built end to end: documents, gap pre-work, evidence questions, translation, writing, chapter. Multi-day resume. A Colombian editor. Three strong learners play it beside today's u45. **If it is not obviously better, stop, and keep the units.** Do not burn 150 of anything before this.

**Stage 4. Supply, four to eight weeks plus burn.** Fares to 300, then 1,000. Reading-pulled intake. Read-only days. Library for placed learners. Relámpago and De corrido. Writing store with lint and avoidance. Reading-pays-reviews as a tested hypothesis.

**Stage 5. The upper rebuild, the largest item.** 150 Expedientes. Three chapters of the book, editor's verdict, then the rest. Afuera, paste with the 30,000-lemma list, Prepárame. The reading yardstick. Segunda parte. The shell through to C1 explanations. Then El tinto, if the checker has earned it.
