# For a native Colombian reader (collected 4 November 2026)

Every script batch was written without a human native pass, by decision. Each writer listed the lines and claims it was least sure of. This is the collected list, by kind, so a Colombian reader can go through it in one sitting. Where a line is wrong, the fix goes in `data/scripts/<band>.js` (search the Spanish) and in `data/natural.js` for the sound-natural items.

## Claims stated as rules or pitfalls

- Invierno means the rainy season in Colombia; Colombia has no summer.
- Te amo is said to parents and children, not only in romance.
- Bocadillo means guava paste; ordinario means crude; vulgar is mostly crude in Latin America.
- Oiga alone sounds rough to a stranger (paisas use it freely).
- Adentro and afuera are what Colombians say when no landmark follows.
- Atascado versus tapado for blocked.
- Lo paso bien is Spain; Colombia says la pasamos bien.
- Hacer parte de as the everyday form; ¿cómo así? for "what do you mean".
- Seguramente glossed as "probably"; after seguramente the ordinary form is the Latin American norm; the subjunctive choice is real only with probablemente and posiblemente.
- De acuerdo a is standard for "according to" in Latin America.
- Deberías ranked stronger than podrías, tendrías que strongest.
- Acuéstate pronto means soon; early is temprano.
- Felices Pascuas is a Christmas greeting.
- Recién llegué and recién me enteré are standard.
- Índice is an index; a rate is tasa.
- El doble de gente vino este año que el año pasado is ungrammatical.
- Me hizo ilusión is Peninsular; the Colombian replacement no me emocionó mucho que digamos.
- Le escribía para sounds like Spain.
- Te agradecería que takes the -ra form, though que me cubras is also heard.
- The article with metaphor nouns: es una fiera said of a man, un gallina switches.
- Me da pena + verb usually means embarrassment; ¿ah, sí? said flat sounds doubtful; ¡ni hablar! means no way.

## Facts stated in hooks and examples

- Colombia has almost half the world's páramos; Bogotá takes most of its water from them.
- The sombrero vueltiao's turn counts and Zenú origin; the mochila wayuu is crocheted by hand from hilo.
- Posta negra is made with panela and vinegar; the Noche de las Velitas dates from the 19th century.
- Monserrate is "over three thousand metres"; the ciclovía draws "more than a million people every Sunday".
- A finca veranda is a corredor; a man's suit is a traje; Calle 45, número 12 as an address.
