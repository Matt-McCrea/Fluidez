# Fluidez: one app, the old one's bones, the best of the rebuild, and the science

Plan written 4 November 2026 from five investigations: a code map of the old app, a code map of the rebuild, a day-by-day read of what an A1 and a B1 learner is actually served by the old app (driven through its own selection code), a content-quality review of the rebuild's story, scripts, scenes and bank, and a research synthesis checked against primary sources. Plan mode blocked the live headless playthroughs; they are Stage 0 below, with the protocol already written.

Decisions you made today: **Latin American Spanish** (the 28 October decision stands; old content is converted). **Model-drafted content through automated gates, no human native pass** (so the gates and a model "native reader" stage have to carry that weight). **Family-and-friends pilot for now** (the post-session report and the league stay).

## 1. Context

Two apps live in one repo on branch `next`: the original vanilla-JS app at the root (`index.html`, `js/`, `data/`), and the TypeScript rebuild in `app/`. You judged the rebuild a failure as a product and the old app the better learner experience, but with lessons that are not elegant for a real beginner. The aim is the best text-only Spanish app for English-native adults, judged by learning effectiveness, not by effort saved.

### What the evidence says about each app

**The old app's bones are right.** Six tabs, everything browsable, a five-stage day (review, learn, read, apply, produce), units that own their days, a lessons browser where every lesson is open, a live writing checklist, eight games, export and import, and lesson prose that is often excellent ("*Gracias* on its own is complete and always enough"). The opening unit's readings are natural and glossed.

**But the old app's day does not cohere, and that is its biggest learning defect.** Traced through its own code (`js/session.js`), day 1 at A1 teaches "two people meet", then drills the present tense of *diseñar*, *nadar* and *insistir* (the present is taught on day 4), then asks the learner to "say something about your training or education". B1 day 1 teaches the subjunctive and not one of its 11 practice items uses it. The review deck tests 7 words on day 1 that were never shown, under the heading "things you have met before", and sits at 27 cards every day from day 2. Thirty-six percent of Apply items repeat within 14 days. In the first fortnight's 28 reading questions, 14 are answerable by picking the longest option. Closing the app after the lesson skips the rest of the day and "Do it again" serves tomorrow. Several A1 passages are vocabulary lists in costume ("Hay soldados, un general y mucho ejército"). The content is Peninsular (vosotros drilled, "the app's voice is peninsular"). Dashes are in nearly every learner-facing string. There is no look-up, no tap-to-gloss, no session persistence, and Leitner boxes instead of a fitted scheduler.

**The rebuild fails as a product, but for reasons that are mostly in the serving layer, not the content.** The content reviewer rated the 916 teaching scripts "the strongest content in the repo" (rules with forms visible, English contrasts, hook before rule). The 219-chapter Bogotá serial holds its plot and is genuinely good from unit 31 on; about a quarter of A1 chapters are bent around a grammar list and the premise lives in English glosses. What makes it feel senseless and repetitive is mechanical: chapters cut into 55-word beats with no recap, the end-of-chapter hook shown after every beat, questions asked before the text that answers them, seven separate re-read paths all labelled "deliberate" so the repeat metric reports zero, scenes replayed timed in the same session, item caps that end A1 days well under their minutes, and a session compiler plus two-card learner model that the audits found failing in 20 distinct ways.

**What to take from the rebuild** (ranked): the TypeScript engine (parity-tested port of `js/engine.js`), the feature tagger and skill graph (6,149 nodes; "what does this sentence require" for any Spanish), the translation matcher with self-correction, the content gates (dashes, duplicates, frames, accent, dialect, option-length bias, coverage, legality), the 72,709-sentence tagged bank with focus spans, the serial and its cast, the scripts, the contrast store (1,493 items), the Look-up search and topic code, the gloss-resolution order, FSRS with implicit credit as a design. Not worth porting as code: the compiler, runner, placement, games.

### The science, in the twelve rules this plan builds on

From the research synthesis (primary sources and meta-analyses; the full note with links is in the session transcript and will be saved as `docs/research/evidence-2026-11-04.md` in Stage 1):

1. Retrieve, never re-read; feedback after every attempt (Rowland 2014, g = 0.50, 0.73 with feedback).
2. Typed recall by default; recognition only as first-exposure scaffolding (cued recall g = 0.61 vs recognition 0.29).
3. Let a fitted model set gaps: FSRS on-device at 0.90 retention (open benchmark, 10k collections); throttle new items on due load, never trim reviews.
4. Two cards per lexeme, meaning and form; form recall decays fastest (Webb et al. 2020: 58% to 25% delayed).
5. Order vocabulary by frequency; compute each text's coverage against this learner's known set; 95% for glossed reading, 98% for free reading (Schmitt, Jiang and Grabe 2011).
6. Four strands every day: read for meaning, write for meaning, study, speed on known material (Nation).
7. Structured input before production for each grammar point (VanPatten and Cadierno 1993), then typed production; rule card under 90 seconds with forms visible (Norris and Ortega; Spada and Tomita).
8. Teach confusable forms one at a time, then interleave across sessions, not within one (Pan et al. 2019 on preterite and imperfect; Nakata and Suzuki 2019). Do not teach tight semantic sets together.
9. Self-correction before the answer: a matched anticipated error gets a one-line hint and one retry (Lyster and Saito 2010; Metcalfe 2017).
10. High-frequency chunks and frames first (*quiero, puedo, tengo que, voy a* + infinitive), tested in new combinations; cognate conversion rules in week 1 (Lubliner and Hiebert 2014).
11. Reading volume is the input strand: large graded library, free choice, words-read as the progress measure (Jeon and Day 2015, adults d = 0.70).
12. Report delayed retention (items 7+ days old), not lesson scores; 15 to 20 minute default with a 5-minute floor and streak repair; aim for about 85 to 90% success per session.

Latin American specifics: preterite as the default past, perfect later for experience and "not yet"; *ustedes* only, vosotros for recognition late; lo/la, no leísmo; every PCIC-derived example gets a Latin American pass.

### What you added on 4 November, and how the plan answers it

**Themes as a weekly choice.** At the end of every unit the app offers next week: "pick a theme, or carry on generally". The list is the 20 PCIC themes at the learner's band that they have not completed at that band. A theme week keeps the grammar ladder exactly where the course puts it (the principle already in `js/focus.js`: "same lesson, different world to meet it in") and swaps everything that was never sequenced: the new-word drip, the readings, the scene, the writing and talk-about prompts (`data/topics.js` already has levelled prompts per topic), the translation set. Each theme has a pack per band: a frequency-ordered word set, 3 to 5 self-contained passages or chapters, 1 or 2 scenes, prompts, a translation set, and a theme check. Completing a theme at a band is recorded in Progress and the same theme reappears one band up with harder material (the topic spiral), so a learner can go deep on what they care about instead of saying a little about everything. Stage 3a builds the choice and the completion record on `Focus` and `UnitCheck`; Stage 7 fills the packs.

**The week as a chain.** A unit is a week of five sessions with different shapes: day 1 the big lesson, day 2 the drill, day 3 reading and theme, day 4 output (scene and writing), day 5 mixed review, the unit check and a "Unit N done" card that says in plain words what you can now do, shows the week's numbers, and offers next week's theme. The serial's chapter for the week is the day 3 reward, and the unit check is the day 5 one. The old course's units already own their days; Stage 6's re-sequence fixes them to this week shape, and Stage 3a ships the completion card and theme offer on the existing units first. This is the blueprint's week and the research's "different practice conditions", not a new idea; the pushback I would give is only that theme weeks must never pause the ladder, which `Focus` already guarantees.

**Coverage must not strangle the writing.** Agreed. The research shows comprehension rises linearly with coverage with no threshold, so a glossed 85 to 90% at A1 is fine where 98% would force telegraphic prose. The rule that replaces the cap is self-containment: every passage and chapter must make sense to fresh eyes, with no scene in the reader's head, whether or not it belongs to a serial. The premise goes in the Spanish, unknown words are glossed inline, cognates and loan words count as known and are used freely at A1 (*hotel, restaurante, café, internet, el teléfono*), and every chapter opens with a one-line "previously" and its cast. Coverage targets by band: A1 85 to 90% glossed, A2 90, B1 93 to 95, B2 and C1 95 to 98 for free reading. The gate gains a fresh-reader check: an Opus job reads the passage cold, writes a one-line summary, and the passage is rejected when that summary does not match the author's intent line. The rebuild's ch001 is rewritten as the exemplar.

**B1 to C1 is where you are, so it gets its own thread** (Stage 5b), running alongside the lesson rewrite: fewer and longer document-led lessons, translation volume from sentence to paragraph, the old app's function and discourse lessons (173 and 33, its real strength at these bands) rebuilt as scripts with register contrasts, the serial's unit 31 to 60 chapters (the ones rated good) available to B1 and above early, "other voices" passages of 250 to 600 words at real-text sentence length (12 to 18 words; the rebuild's C1 mean of 8.4 was too easy), the essay tasks and rubrics that already exist, the contrast store at B1 and above (subjunctive and indicative, preterite and imperfect, the five jobs of *se*), a "sound natural" track in the spirit of Keenan (high-frequency verbs in idiomatic use, anglicism traps), timed re-reads for reading speed, words-read goals per band, and typed production everywhere. You are the first real learner for this thread, and the playtest covers B1 units 1 and 2, one B2 unit and one C1 unit.

**The scheduler's arithmetic has to work, and it must never stall.** Stage 2 states the numbers and tests them: a review budget in minutes per band (A1 6, A2 7, B1 8, B2 10, C1 10) at measured seconds per card; a new-card target per day (A1 8 to 10, falling to 6 at C1) counted in cards, not words; in-session learning steps for a new card (at once, after three other cards, at the end of the session) before FSRS takes over; FSRS at 0.90 gives first intervals of about 1 to 3 days and growth of roughly 2 to 3 times per success; steady state is about 6 to 8 reviews a day per new card a day. The throttle reduces new cards toward a floor of 3 a day and never to zero for more than two consecutive sessions; when due work exceeds twice the budget the app pays it at 1.5 times the budget a day and still introduces 3 new. The simulator walks fast, average and forgetful learners over 663 days and asserts: never three consecutive sessions with no new card, due never above 1.5 times the budget for more than five sessions, at least 30 new words a week at A1, review minutes within budget.

**Accepted answers have to be generous and honest.** Every translate task and typed card carries an accept pattern, not one string: `(a)` for an optional element, `[al|a el]` for alternatives, an optional subject pronoun, accent tolerance with a note, plus the matcher's canonical forms (pronoun drop, clitic position, tú, usted and ustedes, Peninsular accepted and noted). The burn authors at least three accepts per item and the matcher battery verifies them. Grammar-choice items with two right answers are caught by the gate (the rebuild's "one side only" test), and where both really work the card says so ("both work here; *X* is more common in Colombia") instead of marking a miss. Items that turn on a real choice ("can the preposition go here?") carry a one-line note that answers it.

**Playtest more than a fortnight.** The protocol covers A1 days 1 to 30 (two units and a theme week), one A2 unit, B1 units 1 and 2, one B2 unit, one C1 unit, both interruption points, phone and desktop, plus the 663-day simulator, and it is re-driven at every stage end.

## 2. Target architecture

One app, the old one, kept vanilla but given a build step for a shared library.

- **UI stays vanilla JS** in `js/` and `js/views/`, no framework. The shell, tabs, stages, lessons browser, practice, games, Palabras, settings and onboarding are edited in place.
- **A new `lib/` TypeScript package**, bundled by esbuild to `js/lib.bundle.js` and exposed as `window.LIB`, holds what is ported from `app/src`: `engine` (`app/src/engine/conjugate.ts`, `analyze.ts`, `english.ts`, `ortho.ts`, `verbs.json`), `tagger` (`app/src/engine/tagger/*`), the graph loader (`app/src/content/schema.ts` node and edge types), `match` (`app/src/check/translate/*` and `app/src/check/traps/*`), `fsrs` (`ts-fsrs` pinned as in `app/package.json`), `gloss` (the resolution order in `app/src/ui/data/bundle.ts:211-256`), and `search` (`app/src/ui/screens/reference/search.ts`, `topics.ts`, `verbs.ts`). Vitest runs the ported tests, including engine parity against `js/engine.js` (`app/tests/engine/parity.test.ts`). `js/engine.js` stays until parity is green, then `window.ENGINE` is backed by the port.
- **Content stays in `data/*.js`** (window globals), with two additions: `data/graph.js` (the skill graph) and `data/bank/*.js` (the tagged sentence bank, split by band and loaded lazily), plus `data/story.js` (the serial). Every content item gains `requires: [nodeId]` and, where it has a practised form, `focus: [start, end]`, both written by the gate (`npm run gate -- --fix` in `app/`, re-targeted at `data/`).
- **Lazy loading by band.** `data/strand-lessons.js` (3.4 MB) and the bank are split per band; `index.html` loads A1 and the learner's band. `sw.js` `ASSETS` is generated by a tool, not edited by hand (today it must mirror `index.html` by hand; `tools/validate-content.js` already checks staleness).
- **Tools** live in `tools/` (old) and reuse `app/tools/gate`, `app/tools/burn`, `app/tools/contrast`, `app/tools/vocab` by pointing them at `data/`. The rebuild's `app/` directory stays in the repo as the source of ported code and content until Stage 7, then is archived.
- **Branches.** Work on `next` (trunk, per the 1 November ruling R28). `main` is fast-forwarded at the end of each stage; GitHub Pages serves the root of `main`, so the live old app updates only at stage ends. `rebuild` is left where it is; the `fluidez-next` site keeps redeploying only when `rebuild` moves, so it stays frozen at its current build until you decide to retire it.

### Stage 3a. The coherent day: node gating and week 1 (1 to 2 weeks)

- **Unit done, theme next.** When a unit's last day ends, a "Unit N done" card: what you can now do (from the lessons' `canDo` lines), the week's numbers, the unit check (`UnitCheck.run`), and the offer for next week: a theme from the band's uncompleted list, or carry on generally. ## 6. Verification

- **Gates:** `node tools/validate-content.js` plus the ported gate over `data/` at 0 errors; dashes 0; longest-option share within chance plus 8%; list-passage detector 0 on the path; legality 0 (no item requires a node not taught by its day).
- **Library:** vitest green, engine parity green, matcher battery (the audit's 192 valid answers) at or above 90% accepted.
- **Playtest** (the Stage 0 protocol, re-driven each stage): A1 days 1 to 30, one A2 unit, B1 units 1 and 2, one B2 unit, one C1 unit, with these targets: Apply and Produce items 100% within the known set and at least 50% on the day's node; 0 review cards for words not yet shown; repeats within 14 days under 5%; closing the app at any point and reopening resumes the same day; "Do it again" repeats today; screens per A1 day under 40 and measured minutes 15 to 22; no run of more than four cards of one shape; every passage passes the fresh-reader check; no dash on any screen.
- **Simulator:** fast, average and forgetful learners over the full spine: no unknown node served, review minutes within budget, never three consecutive sessions with no new card, due never above 1.5 times the budget for more than five sessions, at least 30 new words a week at A1, calendar length per band reported.
- **Answers:** the matcher battery at or above 90% of valid answers accepted; every translate task and typed card has at least three accepts; zero two-right-answer items unmarked.
- **You:** play day 1 after Stage 1b, A1 units 1 to 3 after Stage 3a and again after Stage 5 before any content scales; play a placed B1 week after Stage 6.
- **Retention:** from Stage 2 the app logs delayed retention locally; after four weeks of your own use it is the number that says whether this worked.

## 7. Risks and what I am assuming

- **No human native pass.** The rebuild's worst prose got through gates alone. The native-reader job and the new detectors are the mitigation; if your own play after Stage 5 still finds unnatural Spanish, the fallback is a human pass on the serial only.
- **The bank was written for the rebuild's course order.** Rows are tagged by node, not by day, so they re-sort onto the old spine; a few will have no legal day until re-sequencing in Stage 6. The supply check reports gaps per node before Stage 3 ships.
- **Lesson-to-node mapping is the load-bearing table.** It is derived automatically and reviewed by a model; errors there show up as misaligned days, which the playtest alignment metric catches.
- **Converting Peninsular content** is lexical and morphological (vosotros, lexis); place names and Spanish characters in the old passages stay unless a passage is rewritten anyway.
- **The report modal and league stay** as asked; the modal could become a button on the completion screen if it annoys testers, and the league's hard-coded publishable key is fine for a pilot.
