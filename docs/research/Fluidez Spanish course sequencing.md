# Teach the Spanish engine first, then select

Fluidez should give learners the core of Spanish grammar in the first three weeks. It should teach the Peninsular pretérito perfecto as the first past tense. And it should use the PCIC the way its authors intended: as a pool to select from, not a list of lessons.

In the restructured course, the irregular high-frequency verbs appear as chunks from day 1 and are unpacked as a system by day 26 (it was day 179). Other key moves:

- Gender and articles come on day 8 (was 99).
- The perfecto comes on day 33 and the indefinido on day 60.
- lo/la come on day 75, and *se lo* on day 142 (was 304).
- Every main indicative tense is in place by day 177.
- The present subjunctive comes on day 181, at the end of A2.
- The imperfect subjunctive comes on day 238 (was 339).

I applied a decision to all 674 lesson ids in the brief: **295 keep, 146 move, 96 merge, 6 split, 125 make optional, 6 cut**. I also added 17 new lessons. The daily path shrinks from 663 days to **537** (A1 107, A2 88, B1 134, B2 112, C1 96). Most of the cut comes from two changes: the 17-lesson B1 emotion run collapses to four lessons, and about 70 C1 idiom-by-notion lessons become an optional vocabulary track.

The evidence for early explicit grammar is strong. The evidence for exact day numbers is not, and the order here leans as much on established Spain-based courses (convention) as on acquisition research.

The hours are the biggest caveat. At the stated 15–30 minutes a day, even the old course gave about 278 hours, against roughly 750 guided hours to reach C1. Fluidez should present itself as a C1 grammar-and-skills spine that requires extensive input on top, and it should raise its daily minutes.

Five uncertainties matter:

1. **Unverified figures.** Several often-quoted effect sizes are marked UNVERIFIED in the notes and are not used here: Norris & Ortega's d ≈ 1.13 vs 0.54, Goo et al.'s g ≈ 1.29 vs 0.77, and Kim & Webb's pooled d.
2. **Unverified PCIC wording.** The PCIC's own statement on whether items within a level are unordered could not be checked. Neither could its exact level for the perfecto, the imperative or *se lo*. The notes confirm only that the past tenses start at A2 ([Muñoz 2022](https://revistes.urv.cat/index.php/rile/article/download/3502/3634/10814)).
3. **Chunks and adults.** The chunk evidence comes from children. No study compares months of phrases first with grammar from week 1 for adults.
4. **Perfecto first.** No study tests teaching the perfecto before the preterite to English speakers.
5. **Other unverified details.** The journal and year for Pan et al., the Nakata & Suzuki title, the primary FSI page, Aula 2 U10 and the Español en marcha unit numbers are all unverified.

App-specific evidence is thin. The closest study is a Duolingo case study with 9 learners, in which gains tracked time on the app ([Loewen et al. 2019](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/abs/mobileassisted-language-learning-a-duolingo-case-study/A4D7C8F71782A37D258C19F357DDBCBE)).

## The revised spine front-loads A1 and paces the contrasts

The draft spine was right about A1 and too fast after it. Fitting 22 tools into 130 days gives about six days per tool at 15 minutes a day: roughly 32 hours for material that Spain-based courses spread over more than 200 guided hours. That leaves no room for the 3–5 spaced reviews and the separate-then-mixed practice of contrasts that the spacing evidence calls for.

The revision therefore pulls the A1 tools to the draft's days or earlier. It paces the hard contrasts (preterite/imperfect, the subjunctive) the way the Spain-based courses do: one new day per structure, plus planned returns. Day numbers are positions in the sequence, so a learner who takes two lessons a day reaches the draft's timeline.

**The open question: yes, teach the present perfect before the preterite.** Three lines of evidence agree:

- **Usage.** The perfecto is becoming the default past in Peninsular Spanish ([Schwenter & Torres Cacoullos 2008](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/BEB0ED75C3979D6BA8BAA077BD464C26), strong evidence). In elicited data it makes up **52.1% of Peninsular past-tense uses, and 61% where no time word is given** ([González et al. 2018](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/isogloss/isogloss_a2018v4n1/isogloss_a2018v4n1p115.pdf), moderate).
- **Learning.** Learners who studied in Spain used the preterite more afterwards but **did not start using the perfecto** ([Blaker & Goebel-Mahrle 2025](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/books/9789027244819-ihll.43.04bla); [Zahler et al. 2023](https://lwc1.benjamins.com/catalog/ihll.37.11zah)). Time in Spain will not fix it later.
- **Convention.** Every Spain-based adult course checked teaches it this way. The form is also easy: *haber*, the 11th most frequent verb, plus a participle.

The counter-arguments are real but weaker. English speakers default to the preterite. No study tests teaching the perfecto first. And the Canary Islands, Galicia, Asturias and León follow the Latin American pattern.

So Fluidez should teach the perfecto on day 33 for *hoy / esta semana / alguna vez*, and the indefinido on day 60 for *ayer / el año pasado*. It should contrast the two explicitly on day 110, mix them in review from then on, and return to them at B1 (day 217) and C1 (day 444). The 27-day gap gives the perfecto three or four spaced reviews before its rival appears.

## Restructuring 674 ids cuts the path to 537 days

The script lays out the new course day by day and applies one decision to every id. Reasons that refer to other lessons take their day numbers from the layout, so they stay correct if the layout changes. For a merged lesson, `new_day` is the day of the lesson it merges into. The CSV has three extra columns: `target` (the merge target or the split parts), `old_day` and `source` (Inventory 2, 3 or 4).

Four decisions do most of the work:

- **The B1 emotion run of 16 lessons becomes 4.** The four lessons are pleasure (fn-me-alegro-b1), worry and sadness (fn-miedo-ansiedad-preocupacion-b1), anger, dislike and indifference (fn-enfado-indignacion-b1), and reacting to others (fn-empatia-b1). The two emotion tasks also merge into one.
- **70 of the 73 C1 notion lessons become an optional C1 vocabulary and idiom track**, fed into spaced review. Three stay on the path because they serve writing: precision, time location and change verbs. Likewise, 38 B2 notion lessons, including the physical-property run you flagged, become an optional B1–B2 vocabulary track.
- **Repeats merge into their first appearance.** This covers the A2/B1 copies of asking for information, directions, adverbs and asking about mood, the B2 copies of the compound tenses, and the paired C1 agree/disagree lessons.
- **Ten unplaced lessons go onto the path where they fill real gaps.** Examples: algo/alguien/nada (day 88), *creo que / dice que* (97), the possessive dative *me duele la cabeza* (143), impersonal *se* for transactions (157), and *es una pena que* (198).

Five of the six cuts are unplaced lessons that classify sentence or verb types; they teach terminology rather than ability. The sixth is fn-destacar-elemento-b1, which is about typography rather than language.

**How your "Place" ratings fared:**

- **"↑ earlier" (51):** 32 moved earlier and 6 merged into earlier lessons. Twelve stay at about the same position, either because they were already in week 1 or because I overrule the draft spine for them. Those are the imperfect; preterite vs imperfect; the preterite itself (day 60 rather than 30, since the perfecto now comes first); and *cuando* + subjunctive, which stays at B1 because future time predicts subjunctive use poorly even in advanced learners. The last one, indirect objects, is systematised later (day 140), but le/me/te are in use from gustar on day 38.
- **"cut/optional" (17):** all honoured — 14 made optional, 2 merged, 1 cut.
- **"merge" (48):** 45 merged; the other 3 are the hub lessons the merges point into.
- **"ok" (448):** 102 became optional (mostly the C1 and B2 notion lists) and 29 merged as duplicates.

**Course length and hours.** The new path is 537 days: A1 days 0–106 (107), A2 107–194 (88), B1 195–328 (134), B2 329–440 (112) and C1 441–536 (96). It holds 399 lessons (382 existing plus 17 new), 28 verb days and 110 practice days.

At the stated minutes (15/20/25/30/30 by band), that is **216 hours** — less than the old 278, and about 29% of the 750-hour Cervantes benchmark. The answer is not to keep the filler. It is to spend the freed time on what builds proficiency:

|---|---|---|---|
| Stated times | 15/20/25/30/30 | 216 | 23–37% |
| Longer sessions | 20/25/30/40/45 | 286 | 31–45% |
| Longer sessions + required extensive input (graded readers, then authentic texts) | 30/45/70/100/120 | ≈654 | A1 59%, A2 55%, B1 104%, B2 104%, C1 91% |

The input figures follow [Nation 2014](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1044345.pdf) and [McQuillan 2016](https://nflrc.hawaii.edu/rfl/item-detail/341). A1 and A2 stay the thinnest bands, and they are where unstructured input helps least, so they are the best place to promote "take another lesson". Unless the input track is tracked and completed, marketing should say "zero to the C1 syllabus", not "zero to C1".

## Short A1 units now carry a grammar tool and a task each

A1 and A2 get 25 new units with new ids (a1-n01 to a2-n25). Each pairs one theme and can-do goal with the grammar it needs, and ends on its task.

- **Practice days.** Every unit has one practice day, used as its mixed-review day and placed 1–3 lessons after the unit's last new grammar.
- **Verb days.** The 28 verb days keep their five-verb sets but are re-ordered to match the grammar. The two irregular sets are on days 1 and 11. The regular -ar/-er/-ir sets come just before the present tense is generalised (days 16 and 40). ## Moving lessons earlier breaks examples and unlocks readings too soon

**Examples that use grammar not yet taught.** Many lessons now come before grammar their current examples assume. Each needs an example audit, and four patterns account for most of them:

- **The perfecto now comes before reflexives and gustar.** At day 33, *me he levantado* and *me ha gustado* are not yet available, so the "Today and ever" examples must avoid reflexive and gustar forms until days 38–52. They can come back in review.
- **B1 and A2 lessons pulled into A1 or early A2 probably contain later grammar.** These lessons are:
  - fn-sensaciones-fisicas-b1 (day 82)
  - fn-preguntar-deseos-b1 (79)
  - gr-perifrasis-verbales-b1 (115)
  - gr-concesivas-b1 (135; strip *aunque* + subjunctive)
  - fn-ofrecer-invitar-b1 and fn-rechazar-b1 (149–150; conditional forms beyond the day-46 polite chunks)
  - gr-relativas-b1 (168; strip subjunctive relatives)
  - gr-pronombres-combinados-b1 (142; strip *dímelo* until clitic placement on day 146)
- **Commands and directions.** The affirmative command now comes on day 91, so nt-movimiento-estabilidad-a1 must not use command forms before then. Its vosotros forms (*girad*) need a check.
- **The imperfect subjunctive at B1** (day 238, was 339) pulls B2-level *si hubiera* examples into B1.

**Readings that unlock too early through tense gating.** Moving a tense earlier unlocks every reading tagged with it. The perfecto unlocks on day 33 instead of 69, the future on day 175 instead of 205, the conditional on 177 instead of 217, and the imperfect subjunctive on 238 instead of 339. Readings that are tense-legal but full of untaught clitics, subjunctive triggers or B2 vocabulary will reach A1–B1 learners. The gate should check more than tense: it needs clitic combinations, mood and triggers, relative clauses and vocabulary frequency band too.

**Merges and splits change identity.** For every merged lesson:

- Its content, spaced-review cards and progress records must move into the target lesson.
- Its id should redirect, not 404.
- The hub lessons, especially the four B1 emotion hubs and the C1 hubs, need rewriting rather than concatenation, or they will rebuild the overload problem.

The six split lessons, split from presente-irregular, preterito, pronombres-atonos, ser-estar, por-para and referencias-generales, must not carry their whole old content forward.

**Existing learners need a migration.** Map each learner's position by the set of lessons they have completed, not by day number. Someone at old day 120 has the imperfect but not the irregular present or the perfecto as currently ordered. Backfill those as catch-up lessons rather than resetting anyone.

**Production-gated mastery would fail.** Predicative agreement, 3rd-person pronouns, estar + adjective and the subjunctive are late in development. Gate on comprehension, and track production as a lagging measure. Don't count a correct chunk as mastery; test with new combinations.

**What to check.** Most of these checks are automatable:

1. Tag every example and reading for tense, mood, pronoun sequence, relative clauses and command forms. Flag anything above the learner's position.
2. Re-run the gating with the extra features above.
3. Check that each grammar point gets at least 4–5 scheduled reviews in its first two months, with the last gap at least 3–4 weeks.
4. Check that contrast pairs (perfecto/indefinido, preterite/imperfect, ser/estar, por/para) appear mixed in review only once both members have been taught.
5. Regenerate the machine-written can-do text for the C1 grammar lessons.
6. Mark leísmo (*le veo*) as accepted Peninsular usage from the lo/la lesson onwards.

## Conclusion

The restructure turns the PCIC from a script into a parts bin, and two consequences follow. First, the hardest decisions are no longer about A1, where research, frequency and the Spain-based courses all agree. They are about pacing from A2 onwards, where Fluidez's daily minutes, not its lesson order, set the ceiling. Second, a shorter, denser path makes the hours gap more visible, and that is useful: the 126 days the redesign frees are the natural place for the extensive reading and listening track that C1 needs. On this evidence, the measure of whether the redesign works is not "taught by day N". It is whether learners can still comprehend and choose a form three months after they were taught it, so analytics should measure delayed retrieval and the accuracy of new recombinations, not lesson completion.
