### task-c1-ironia [task C1] Di lo contrario de lo que quieres decir
SEQ: move day=479
canDo: be ironic in Spanish, and hear it done to you
summary: Irony is the last thing a learner acquires and the first thing that marks fluency, because it requires knowing exactly what the neutral version would have been. Spanish marks it — with intonation, with a suspended "que", with an adjective that is too generous.
keywords: ¡Qué sorpresa! = What a shock. (said when it isn't) | Pues sí que estamos bien. = Well, this is just great. | Un detalle… = How thoughtful… (when it wasn't) | con tanta antelación = with so much notice | Ya, claro. = Right, sure. | No, si encima… = And on top of that…
- What to do: Write a conversation of twenty turns between two people who know each other well, in which at least six lines mean the opposite of what they say.

### dc-atenuacion-c1 [discourse C1] Atenuación: decirlo sin señalar a nadie
SEQ: keep day=457
canDo: handle atenuación at C1
summary: At C1 the difficulty is rarely the grammar of a criticism, a refusal or an order — it is saying it without the sentence pointing a finger. Spanish softens by MOVING THE PERSON: what is really about you becomes “we”, and what is really about me becomes “one” or nobody at all.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:288 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:289 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:291 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:295
keywords: ¿Y si…? = What if…? | Vamos a ver qué hemos hecho. = Let's see what we've done. (said to one person) | Habría que… = Someone ought to… | uno = one (meaning I, at a distance) | se diría que… = you'd say that… | quizá convendría… = it might be advisable to… | merecer la pena = to be worth it
- The mechanism: Every direct statement names an agent: Tienes que revisarlo names you, Creo que te equivocas names me and you. Atenuación displaces that agent onto a less exposed person — first plural, an impersonal se, or nobody — so the criticism survives but the accusation does not.
- Moving “you” to “we”: The plural sociativo makes your instruction a shared task: ¿Y si lo dejamos para mañana? means leave it, but nobody has been told to do anything. Teachers, doctors and managers live in this construction: Vamos a ver qué hemos hecho aquí.
- Moving “I” to nobody: An opinion becomes a fact of the world: Habría que revisarlo, Se recomienda esperar, Cabría preguntarse si…. In writing this is the default register of reports and journalism, where a named opinion sounds like an outburst.
- The cost: Atenuación is not politeness for its own sake — it buys the other person a way out. Overused it reads as evasive, and in Spanish, as in English, a manager who never says you is not trusted.
exponents: ¿Y si lo dejamos para mañana? [plural sociativo — really means “you leave it”, but names no one] | Vamos a ver qué hemos hecho aquí. [the teacher/parent “we”; the error is entirely the listener's] | Habría que revisarlo. [obligation with no subject at all — softer than “tienes que”] | Uno acaba pensando que no merece la pena. [“uno” generalises a private opinion into a shared experience] | No sé si me explico. [moves the blame for any confusion onto the speaker] | Quizá convendría replantear el enfoque. [impersonal + conditional; standard in meetings] | Se ruega no fumar en las instalaciones. [impersonal se — the prohibition has no author] | Cabría preguntarse si la medida resulta proporcionada. [the standard way to raise an objection in written argument]
pitfalls: Atenuación is not the conditional alone. Podrías revisarlo is still aimed at you; habría que revisarlo is aimed at nobody. The softening comes from losing the subject, not from the tense. | The sociative we is warm from a colleague and patronising from a stranger — ¿Cómo estamos hoy? to an adult patient is famously grating. | In written argument, yo creo que is not wrong but is weak; cabría preguntarse and todo apunta a que carry far more weight for the same claim.

### dc-reformuladores-c1 [discourse C1] Reformuladores: volver a decirlo mejor
SEQ: keep day=462
canDo: handle reformuladores at C1
summary: A reformulador announces that what follows replaces what preceded — and the class you pick tells the listener WHY you are saying it again: to explain it, to sum it up, to correct yourself, or to declare the whole preceding discussion irrelevant. Choosing the wrong class is not a style slip; it misreports your own intention.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:22 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:23 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:24 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:25
keywords: o sea = that is, in other words | es decir = that is to say | dicho de otro modo = put another way | mejor dicho = or rather (correcting yourself) | en otras palabras = in other words | más bien = rather | no sostenerse = not to hold up
- Four jobs, not one: Spanish separates what English blurs into "I mean". Explicativos (es decir, o sea, dicho de otro modo) restate the same content more clearly. Recapitulativos (en resumidas cuentas, al fin y al cabo) compress several points into a conclusion. Rectificativos (mejor dicho, más bien) withdraw what you just said and replace it. De distanciamiento (en todo caso, de cualquier modo) declare the preceding argument beside the point.
- The distancing ones are the C1 move: De todos modos and en cualquier caso do something no English "anyway" quite does: they concede that everything said so far may be true and rule it irrelevant to the conclusion. —Es caro, y además está lejos. —En cualquier caso, es la única opción que tenemos. The objections are not answered; they are set aside.
- Rectifying is not hedging: Mejor dicho replaces; it does not soften. Llegó tarde, mejor dicho, no llegó withdraws "late" and asserts something stronger. Learners often reach for it when they mean bueno or en realidad, which weaken rather than replace.
- Register spread: O sea is heavily colloquial and, overused, marks a speaker as vague. Es decir is neutral and safe anywhere. Dicho de otro modo and en otras palabras belong to writing and prepared speech. Esto es is markedly formal and almost only written.
exponents: O sea, que no vienes. [explicativo; very frequent in speech and easy to overuse] | Es decir, tendremos que empezar de nuevo. [the safe default explicativo] | Dicho de otro modo, el plan no se sostiene. [prepared speech and writing] | Llegó tarde; mejor dicho, no llegó. [rectificativo: withdraws and replaces, does not soften] | Más bien diría lo contrario. [rectifies someone else, gently] | En resumidas cuentas, no salió como esperábamos. [recapitulativo] | Al fin y al cabo, nadie le obligó a firmar. [recapitulativo that appeals to something obvious] | En cualquier caso, la decisión ya está tomada. [de distanciamiento: concedes the argument and rules it irrelevant] | Esto es, el contrato quedó sin efecto. [markedly formal, almost only written]
pitfalls: O sea is not a neutral es decir. In an interview or a report it reads as imprecise; used every second sentence it reads as a filler. | Mejor dicho REPLACES what you said. If you only want to soften it, you want bueno, en realidad or más o menos — reaching for mejor dicho tells the listener your first statement was wrong. | The distancing reformuladores (en todo caso, de cualquier modo) do not answer an objection. Using one where an answer is expected can read as evasive, which is sometimes exactly the intention — but know which you are doing.

### dc-estilo-indirecto-c1 [discourse C1] Estilo indirecto: el verbo que interpreta
SEQ: keep day=461
canDo: handle estilo indirecto at C1
summary: Reporting speech at C1 is not a mechanical shift of tenses and pronouns. The announcing verb you choose — dijo, aclaró, reprochó, advirtió, soltó — tells the listener how to take what follows, and that judgment is yours, not the original speaker's. Report neutrally and you have chosen too; you have chosen not to comment.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:79 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:80 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:81 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:83 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:84 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:85

- The verb carries your reading: Every announcing verb beyond decir adds an interpretation the original speaker never signed up to. Me aclaró que no vendría presents it as clearing something up; me reprochó que no fuera as an accusation; me advirtió que no fuera as a warning; me soltó que no vendría makes it abrupt and unwelcome. Same words reported, four different events.
- Verbs that cannot report: Some verbs only introduce direct speech: you can cantar, recitar or pronunciar words, but not cantar que something is the case. *Le cantó que era morena is not Spanish; it has to be Le cantó: «Eres morena».

Interjections do not survive intact — they are absorbed into the clause: María dijo: «¡Ay, qué susto!» reports as María dijo que ay, qué susto.
exponents: Me dijo que llegaría al día siguiente. [the neutral report; note mañana > al día siguiente] | Me aclaró que no había firmado nada. [presents the utterance as resolving a doubt] | Me advirtió que no volviera a llamar. [advertir + subjunctive: the report is a warning] | Me reprochó que no hubiera ido a la reunión. [reprochar makes the reported words an accusation] | Me soltó que ya se encargaría otro. [soltar marks the utterance as blunt and unwelcome] | El canalla de Alberto me dijo que lo llamara hoy mismo. [the evaluative phrase is the reporter's verdict, not the speaker's] | Los abajo firmantes hacen constar que no fueron informados. [hacer constar: the reporting formula of official writing]
pitfalls: Deixis has to move with the tense. Me dijo que venía mañana only works if mañana is still in the future when you speak; otherwise it is al día siguiente. | An imperative reported becomes the subjunctive, not an infinitive: «Llámame» → me dijo que lo llamara, never *me dijo llamarlo. | Choosing a loaded announcing verb is a claim you are making. Reprochar, soltar and insinuar will be read as your characterisation, and in writing you can be held to it.

### dc-citas-encubiertas-c1 [discourse C1] Citas encubiertas: decirlo sin responder de ello
SEQ: keep day=456
canDo: handle citas encubiertas at C1
summary: Spanish can mark information as second-hand without any verb of saying at all — most strikingly by putting a present or future event into the imperfect. "Ernesto llegaba mañana" says the same as "llegará", except that the speaker is passing on what they were told and declines to vouch for it. This is invisible to a learner who reads the imperfect as a past tense.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:86 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:87 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:88 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:89 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:90 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:91
keywords: Llegaba mañana. = He's arriving tomorrow (or so I'm told). | según me dicen = so I'm told | por lo visto = apparently | al parecer = apparently | dicen que… = they say that… | se rumorea que… = it's rumoured that… | responder de algo = to vouch for something
- The imperfect that is not past: Ernesto llegaba mañana de París. The event is in the future; the imperfect is doing something else entirely. It marks the statement as reported — I was told this — and quietly withholds the speaker's commitment. Compare Ernesto llegará mañana, which the speaker asserts.
- The same trick over a past event: It works over the preterite too: Ernesto llegó ayer. Comía con Julia y se quedaba en su casa. The first sentence is asserted; the two imperfects are relayed. A listener hears exactly where the speaker's own knowledge stops.
- Naming the source without naming it: según me dicen, según parece, por lo visto, al parecer, en palabras de all attribute without committing. Por lo visto and al parecer are the workhorses of Spanish journalism for exactly this reason.
- Conceding a quotation in order to reject it: The future can quote an opinion and grant it before objecting: —Marta es muy lista. —Será muy lista, pero no ha resuelto el problema. Será here is not future at all; it concedes what the other person said. Aunque + subjunctive does the same work: Aunque sea muy tarde, tenemos que terminarlo.
exponents: Ernesto llegaba mañana de París. [imperfect for a future event: relayed, not vouched for] | Comía con Julia y se quedaba en su casa. [imperfect over a past event, marking it second-hand] | Ya han terminado, según me dicen. | Por lo visto, la reunión se ha aplazado. [the everyday evidential; extremely common] | Al parecer, no se informó a los afectados. [the press formula for an unconfirmed claim] | En palabras del propio autor, se trata de un experimento. | Será muy lista, pero no ha resuelto el problema. [concessive future: quotes the claim and grants it before objecting] | Aunque sea muy tarde, tenemos que terminarlo. [aunque + subjunctive concedes a point already made by someone else]
pitfalls: The relaying imperfect is not a past tense and must not be translated as one. Ernesto llegaba mañana is about tomorrow. | It carries a real disclaimer. If you assert something yourself and use it, you will be heard as distancing yourself from your own statement — and later as having hedged deliberately. | Será muy lista, pero… is not a prediction. Reading it as future is the commonest misparse of this construction; the clue is that a pero is almost always coming.

### dc-atenuacion-dialogica-c1 [discourse C1] Atenuación dialógica: discrepar sin romper nada
SEQ: keep day=458
canDo: handle atenuación dialógica at C1
summary: Disagreement in Spanish is almost never delivered flat. It is prefaced — by a partial agreement, an admission of ignorance, or an apology for what is about to be said — and the preface is not decoration: it is what keeps the exchange open. Skipping it does not sound direct, it sounds hostile.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:313 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:314 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:315 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:316
keywords: Bueno, no creas… = Well, I wouldn't say so… | Sí, bueno, pero… = Yes, well, but… | Hombre, yo diría que… = Well, I'd say that… | No sé, ¿eh? = I don't know, though. | Ya, pero… = Right, but… | Sin ánimo de ofender… = With no offence meant… | discrepar = to disagree
- Agree partially, then turn: The standard move grants something before withdrawing it: No, no es eso exactamente, es que…, Sí, bueno, pero…, Yo no digo que esté mal, pero yo no lo haría. That last frame is worth learning whole: no digo que + subjunctive denies an accusation you have not been given, then states the objection.
- Plead ignorance rather than assert error: Instead of "you are wrong", C1 Spanish reaches for No sé, la verdad es que yo no diría eso or Puede que me equivoque, pero…. The speaker relocates the disagreement into their own uncertainty. It is a fiction and everyone knows it; declining to use it is what gets noticed.
- Minimising the other person's claim: Bueno, no creas, hombre, tanto como eso no and no es para tanto shrink a claim rather than contradicting it. —La carretera estará sin asfaltar. —Bueno, no creas, está bastante bien.
- Apologising in advance: Formal registers preface the disagreement outright: Sin ánimo de contradecirle, creo que se equivoca, Con todos mis respetos, Si me permite la observación. These are stronger than they look — they announce that disagreement is coming and make it impossible to take as an accident.

Used among friends it sounds sarcastic, which may be the point, but know that it will land that way.

### dc-metaforas-c1 [discourse C1] Metáforas: el juicio escondido en la gramática

summary: Spanish carries evaluation in constructions, not just in adjectives. "Marta es un plomo" is not a description with a colourful noun — the indefinite article plus an evaluative noun IS the judgment, and there is no neutral way to say it. Learners who translate the words and miss the frame produce Spanish that is grammatical and expressionless.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:147 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:148 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:149 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:150 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:151

- Frozen metaphors: A large part of everyday Spanish is metaphor that has stopped being felt as such: morirse de risa, llover a mares, tomar el pelo, tener los pies en la tierra, costar un ojo de la cara. These are not optional colour; a C1 speaker who avoids them sounds translated.

### dc-imperfecto-modal-c1 [discourse C1] El imperfecto que no habla del pasado
SEQ: move day=443

summary: Beyond narration, the imperfect does jobs that have nothing to do with past time: it excuses, it registers surprise, it softens a request. "¡Y yo qué sabía!" is not a statement about what you once knew — it is a defence. These uses are frequent in speech and almost never taught, so learners hear a past tense and lose the speaker's attitude entirely.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:271 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:272 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:273 tacticas_pragmaticas:C1:274
keywords: ¡Y yo qué sabía! = How was I supposed to know! | ¿No te habías ido? = Hadn't you left? (surprise, not past) | ¿Tú no tenías un examen mañana? = Don't you have an exam tomorrow? - Imperfecto de excusa: ¡Y yo qué sabía! — "How was I supposed to know!" The imperfect relocates the speaker to the moment before the problem, where their ignorance was reasonable. It is a defence against blame, not a report.
- Imperfecto de sorpresa: Meeting an expectation that has been overturned: Anda, estabas aquí; ¿no te habías ido? or ¿Tú no tenías un examen? The imperfect marks what the speaker had assumed, and the surprise lies in the gap between that and what they now see. With disapproval it becomes an accusation dressed as a question.

[imperfecto de cortesía: distances the speaker from their own request] | Quería preguntarle una cosa. [the standard polite opener; the present would be blunt] | Como se haya olvidado, no llegamos a tiempo. [como + futuro perfecto/subjunctive: conjecture as a threat] | Le escribía para interesarme por el estado del expediente. [the epistolary imperfect — present in meaning, distanced in form]

summary: A review that only summarizes the plot has failed at its one job: judging. A C1 reseña states a clear verdict, backs it with specific evidence from the work itself, and closes with a recommendation that names who the work is (or isn't) for — not just whether it was "good".

- Para qué sirve: Una reseña no es un resumen — es un juicio razonado que ayuda a un lector a decidir si le merece la pena acercarse a una obra (libro, película, disco, exposición). El resumen es solo el contexto mínimo necesario para que el juicio se entienda.
