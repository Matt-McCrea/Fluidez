### task-b1-fisico [task B1] Describe algo con precisión
SEQ: keep day=328
canDo: describe an object precisely — size, shape, material, state and where it is
summary: Vague description is the mark of a B1 learner who has the words but not the patterns. This unit is the patterns: estar a for measurement, estar hecho de for material, estar + participio for the state something has been left in.
keywords: medir = to measure, to be … tall | pesar = to weigh | caber = to fit | estar hecho de = to be made of | la forma = the shape | metros cuadrados = square metres | estar a … grados = to be … degrees
- What to do: Describe three things precisely enough that someone could pick them out of a pile: something you own, something in the room, and something you would buy. Ten to fourteen sentences in total.
- Use the patterns, not just the words: Each description must carry at least three of: mide / mide … metros, está a … grados, está hecho de …, está + participio (roto, pintado, envuelto), cabe / no cabe, está lleno / vacío / libre / ocupado.
- The distinction to get right: Lleno and vacío say whether a space has anything in it. Libre and ocupado say whether it is available to you. A full train can still have a free seat, so el tren está lleno and hay un asiento libre are both true at once.

### task-b1-opinion [task B1] Defiende una opinión
SEQ: keep day=205
canDo: state an opinion and say why, including what you do NOT think
summary: The subjunctive arrives here as a meaning contrast, not a paradigm. "Creo que es" and "no creo que sea" differ by one mood, and that mood is the whole difference between asserting something and declining to.
keywords: En mi opinión… = In my opinion… | Creo que… = I think that… | No creo que… = I don't think that… (+ subjunctive) | Estoy de acuerdo. = I agree. | Por un lado… por otro = On the one hand… on the other | Además = What's more | En conclusión = In conclusion
- What to do: Pick something you actually have a view on. Write twelve to sixteen sentences: your position, three reasons, and at least two things you do NOT think.

### task-b1-debate [task B1] Discute con alguien que no está de acuerdo
SEQ: keep day=214
canDo: hold a disagreement without it becoming a row
summary: Agreement in Spanish is a scale, and so is disagreement. The useful skill at B1 is partial agreement — conceding something real before you push back, which is what stops a disagreement sounding like a rejection.
keywords: estar a favor / en contra = to be for / against | Sí, pero… = Yes, but… | por un lado / por otro = on the one hand / on the other | además = besides | sin embargo = however | en mi opinión = in my opinion | para terminar = to finish
- What to do: Write a twelve-turn conversation between two people who disagree. Neither may simply repeat themselves; each turn has to move.

### task-b1-consejo [task B1] Aconseja a alguien que te lo pide
SEQ: keep day=237
canDo: give advice at three levels of directness
summary: The conditional is the politeness tense. Advice given in it leaves the other person room to ignore you, which is exactly what advice should do.

### task-b1-deseos [task B1] Di lo que quieres que pase
SEQ: move day=186
canDo: say what you want someone else to do, and what you hope will happen

### task-b1-planes [task B1] Cuenta tus planes y predice algo
SEQ: move day=180
canDo: talk about the future, and guess about the present
summary: Spanish has two futures doing different jobs. Ir a is what you have decided; the simple future is prediction — and the same form, oddly, is how Spanish guesses about right now.
keywords: pienso + infinitivo = I'm planning to | voy a + infinitivo = I'm going to | cuando + subjuntivo = when (in the future) | seguramente = probably | me gustaría = I'd like | el año que viene = next year | si todo va bien = if all goes well

### task-b1-pedir [task B1] Pide, niégate y prohíbe
SEQ: move day=269

summary: B1 is where requests stop being formulas and start being choices. The same favour asked four ways is four different relationships.
keywords: ¿Podrías…? = Could you…? | ¿Te importaría…? = Would you mind…? | Necesito que… = I need you to… | Es que… = The thing is… | Lo siento, no puedo. = Sorry, I can't. | por favor = please | Gracias de todos modos. = Thanks anyway.

### task-b1-relato [task B1] Cuenta una historia con un antes

### task-b1-escribir [task B1] Escribe una composición

### dc-intensificacion-discurso-b1 [discourse B1] Intensificar: el más... de, -ísimo, kilómetros y kilómetros

summary: Repeating a word (kilómetros y kilómetros) intensifies exactly like -ísimo does, just by a completely different mechanism — piling up the word itself instead of piling up morphology onto it.

- Superlativo comparativo de excelencia: El/la más... de singles someone or something out from a whole group: Joaquín es, sin duda, el más antipático de la clase.

### dc-focalizacion-b1 [discourse B1] Focalización: solo, sobre todo, ¡qué bien habla!
SEQ: merge day=384 target=dc-focalizacion-b2
summary: Sólo voy yo narrows the sentence down to one fact — that it is only me going, nobody else — and Spanish has a small toolkit of words and structures whose entire job is to point at which piece of the sentence matters.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:182 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:183 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:184 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:185 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:186 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:187
- Excluyentes: solamente, solo: Restricts to exactly one thing, excluding everything else: Solo voy yo — nobody else is going.
- Particularizadores: sobre todo: Singles out the main case within a broader category, without excluding the rest: Me gusta leer, sobre todo libros de viajes — travel books are the favorite, but not the only thing read.

### dc-negacion-refuerzo-b1 [discourse B1] Refuerzo de la negación: nadie, hasta, desde
SEQ: merge day=219 target=dc-estructuradores-a2
summary: Some negative words are only grammatical AFTER "no", and two prepositions most learners think of as purely positive — hasta and desde — turn out to require a negated verb whenever the clause they introduce describes a single, non-repeatable event.
pcic: tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:136 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:137 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:138 tacticas_pragmaticas:B1:139
- Postverbal negative words need "no": Nadie, nunca, ninguno after the verb require no before it: Luis no ha hablado de esto con nadie nunca. Without no, the sentence is simply ungrammatical: *Luis ha hablado de esto con nadie nunca.

[veiled negation — no explicit "no" at all]
pitfalls: Never drop the preceding no just because nadie/nunca/ninguno already sound negative — Spanish requires both, unlike English "never...anyone" which needs only one negative word. | Hasta and desde only force negation with a NON-DURATIVE predicate (a single point, like "arrive" or "leave") — a durative predicate (like "estar aquí") behaves differently and does not have this restriction. | "Es que..." as veiled negation only works as a refusal when the listener can infer the "no" from context — in writing or with someone who cannot see the implication, an explicit refusal is clearer.
