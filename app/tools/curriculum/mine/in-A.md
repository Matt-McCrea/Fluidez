### nt-familia-a1 [notion A1] La familia: quién es quién
SEQ: move day=23
canDo: say who is in your family, how many there are, and how old they are
summary: Spanish names a mixed group with the masculine plural — "mis hermanos" is brothers, or brothers and sisters together, and there is no way to tell from the word alone. That one habit runs through the whole family vocabulary and explains half of what looks irregular in it.
keywords: el hermano / la hermana = the brother / the sister | los hermanos = the siblings (or the brothers) | el padre / la madre = the father / the mother | los padres = the parents | los abuelos = the grandparents | el hijo / la hija = the son / the daughter | la pareja = the partner | mayor = older | menor = younger | hijo único = an only child
- The masculine plural covers the mixed group: One brother and one sister are mis hermanos. A mother and a father are mis padres. Grandparents of both sexes are mis abuelos (my grandparents). The feminine plural is reserved for an all-female group: mis hermanas means sisters only. So tengo tres hermanos tells you the number and nothing about who they are — a Spanish speaker asking for more will say ¿chicos o chicas? — boys or girls?
- Tener, not ser: Family membership uses tener: tengo dos hermanos, tiene tres hijos. English says "there are four of us"; Spanish says somos cuatro — with ser, and with no word for "of us" at all. Both are worth learning as fixed shapes.
- Mayor and menor, not grande and pequeño: For siblings, age is mayor (older) and menor (younger): mi hermana mayor — my older sister. Más grande would be about size. Pequeño is used affectionately for a much younger sibling — mi hermano pequeño — but menor is the neutral word.
- The question you will be asked: ¿Tienes hermanos? is one of the first things anyone asks. The full answer has three parts and all three are short: how many, what they are, and roughly their age. Sí, dos: una hermana mayor y un hermano pequeño.
exponents: ¿Tienes hermanos? [the standard opener] | Somos cuatro en casa. [ser, and no word for "of us"] | Tengo una hermana mayor. [mayor, not más grande] | Soy hijo único. [hija única for a woman] | Mis padres están separados. [estar, because it is a state] | Vivo con mi pareja. [pareja is neutral as to sex] | ¿Y tus abuelos, viven cerca?
contrasts: mis hermanos (masculine plural — a mixed group, or all male) | mis hermanas (feminine plural — all female only) | mi hermano mayor (age) | mi hermano grande (not what you meant)
pitfalls: Tengo 30 años, never soy 30 — age is something you HAVE in Spanish. | Somos cinco for "there are five of us". Adding a word for "of us" is the commonest slip here. | Mis padres is my parents. My fathers, in the plural-of-father sense, is not a thing the word does — and parientes means relatives, not parents.

### task-a2-ayer [task A2] Cuenta qué hiciste el fin de semana
SEQ: move day=65
canDo: tell someone what you did, in order, in the past
summary: The preterite is the tense of "and then". Its whole job is to move a story forward one completed event at a time, which is why a first attempt at it should be a sequence and nothing else.
keywords: primero = first | luego = then | después = afterwards | más tarde = later | al final = in the end | desayunar = to have breakfast | salir a correr = to go out for a run | el mercado = the market | cenar = to have dinner
- What to do: Write eight to twelve sentences about a real weekend or day off, in the order things happened. Every verb in the preterite. No descriptions yet — just what happened.
- Use the joins: Primero, luego, después, más tarde, al final. Without them a sequence reads as a list; with them it reads as a story. At least four of your sentences should start with one.
- The three that break: Ser and ir share the same preterite (fui), and context alone tells them apart. Hacer gives hice/hizo — note the c→z. Expect to get these wrong twice before they stick.

### task-a2-antes [task A2] Cómo era tu vida hace diez años
SEQ: keep day=119
canDo: describe how things used to be, with no interest in when they stopped
summary: The imperfect is not "the other past tense" — it is a different question. The preterite answers what happened; the imperfect answers what things were like. Writing a whole text in it is the fastest way to feel the difference.
keywords: antes = before, back then | vivía = I used to live | tenía = I had, I used to have | era = it was, I was | hace diez años = ten years ago | ya no = not any more | el pueblo = the town, village | cambiar = to change
- What to do: Describe your life ten years ago, or your childhood, in eight to twelve sentences. Every verb in the imperfect. Deliberately nothing completed — no single events at all.
- What the imperfect is for: Habits (iba todos los días), descriptions (la casa era pequeña), age (tenía diez años), ongoing states (no me gustaba). None of these has a start or an end in view.
- Only three are irregular: Ser (era), ir (iba), ver (veía). Everything else takes -aba or -ía with no exceptions at all — the easiest tense in Spanish to form.

### task-a2-anecdota [task A2] Cuenta una anécdota
SEQ: keep day=123
canDo: tell a story that has both a background and a sequence of events
summary: This is the unit the last two were building towards. A real anecdote needs both tenses at once: the imperfect paints the situation, the preterite drops the events into it. Using only one of them is what makes a learner story sound flat.
keywords: una vez = once | estaba = I was (in the middle of something) | cuando = when | de repente = suddenly | sonar = to ring | al final = in the end | la cocina = the kitchen | el teléfono = the phone
- What to do: Tell something that actually happened to you, in twelve to sixteen sentences. It must open with a situation in the imperfect before anything happens.
- The shape: Set the scene (imperfect: where, when, weather, who, how you felt). Then the events (preterite). Then a closing reaction. Most learner anecdotes skip straight to the events, which is why they read like a list.
- Interrupting: Estaba en la cocina cuando sonó el teléfono. — I was in the kitchen when the phone rang. The imperfect is what was going on; the preterite cuts across it. This sentence pattern is worth learning as a shape.

### task-a2-experiencia [task A2] ¿Alguna vez has…?
SEQ: move day=37
canDo: ask and answer about experience — what you have and have not done
summary: The present perfect is not a third past tense to choose between. It answers a different question: not when, but whether it has happened at all, at any point up to now.
keywords: ¿Has estado alguna vez…? = Have you ever been…? | ¿Has probado…? = Have you tried…? | Sí, he… = Yes, I have… | No, nunca. = No, never. | ya = already | todavía no = not yet | una vez = once | dos veces = twice
- What to do: Write an interview of eight exchanges about experience. Every question uses ¿Has…? or ¿Alguna vez has…?; every answer uses he, nunca he, ya or todavía no.
- Then break out of it: Once an answer is yes, the follow-up question almost always switches to the preterite, because now you are asking WHEN and WHERE. —¿Has estado en México? —Sí. —¿Cuándo fuiste? That switch is the point of the unit.
- The participles that break: Hacer→hecho, ver→visto, escribir→escrito, volver→vuelto, decir→dicho, poner→puesto. There are about a dozen and they are all common.

### task-a2-pronombres [task A2] Reescríbelo sin repetir el nombre
SEQ: move day=145
canDo: replace nouns with pronouns so you stop repeating yourself
summary: Every learner writes "Compré el libro y leí el libro y le di el libro a mi hermana." Spanish speakers say it once. This unit is the difference.
keywords: lo / la / los / las = it, him, her, them | le / les = to him, to her, to them | se lo / se la = it to him, it to her | comprar = to buy | leer = to read | dar = to give | mañana = tomorrow
- What to do: Write a short paragraph deliberately repeating one noun four or five times. Then rewrite it with pronouns. Keep both.
- Which pronoun: Lo, la, los, las replace the thing the verb acts on. Le, les replace the person it goes TO. Se lo di — when both appear, le becomes se, which has no logic and simply has to be learned.
- Where they go: Before a conjugated verb (lo compré), or stuck onto an infinitive or gerund (voy a comprarlo / lo voy a comprar — both are correct).

### task-a2-pedir [task A2] Pide lo mismo de cinco maneras
SEQ: move day=153
canDo: make the same request from blunt to very polite, and know which is which
summary: Spanish politeness is not a word you add. It is a scale built out of tense, person and question form, and moving up it changes how much room you leave the other person to say no.
keywords: ¿Puedes…? = Can you…? | ¿Podría…? = Could you…? | por favor = please | Claro. = Of course. | Lo siento, no puedo. = Sorry, I can't. | Es que… = The thing is… | Gracias. = Thank you.
- What to do: Take one request — a window opened, a form explained, a day off. Write it five ways, from the bluntest to the most careful, and label each with who you would say it to.
- The rungs: Imperative (Abre la ventana) · present question (¿Abres la ventana?) · poder (¿Puedes abrir…?) · conditional (¿Podrías…?) · impersonal distance (¿Se podría…?). Each rung removes you a little further from ordering someone about.
- Softening the refusal too: Saying no needs the same care: Es que…, Lo siento, pero…, Me temo que no va a poder ser. A flat no is much harsher in Spanish than its English equivalent.

### task-a2-animo [task A2] Reacciona a cinco noticias
SEQ: move day=167
canDo: react to good and bad news the way a person actually does
summary: Silence is the beginner default and it reads as indifference. Spanish expects an audible reaction, and the reaction is usually shorter than learners expect — two or three words, immediately.
keywords: ¿Qué tal estás? = How are you? | Estoy… = I am… | Tengo… = I have… (hambre, sed, sueño) | Me duele… = … hurts | ¡Que te mejores! = Get well soon! | ¡Enhorabuena! = Congratulations! | ¡Qué pena! = What a shame!
- What to do: Write five short exchanges. Somebody tells you something; you react. Two pieces of good news, two bad, one ambiguous.
- Match the size: Big news gets ¡No me digas! or ¡Qué fuerte!. Small news gets ¡Ah, qué bien!. Using the big one for small news sounds sarcastic, which is a mistake learners make constantly.
- Then ask: A reaction on its own closes the conversation. A reaction plus a question keeps it open: —Me han dado el trabajo. —¡Enhorabuena! ¿Cuándo empiezas?

### task-a2-comparar [task A2] Compara dos opciones y elige
SEQ: move day=131
canDo: compare two things and justify which you would pick
summary: Comparison is where an opinion stops being a preference and starts being an argument. It also carries four irregular forms that no rule produces.
keywords: más… que = more… than | menos… que = less… than | tan… como = as… as | mejor / peor = better / worse | parecido a = similar to | la diferencia = the difference | en cambio = on the other hand
- What to do: Pick two real things — two cities, two phones, two jobs. Compare them across four points, then choose one and say why.
- The three frames: More/less: más caro que, menos caro que. The same: tan caro como, igual de caro que. The most: el más caro de todos.
- The four that break: Bueno→mejor, malo→peor, grande→mayor, pequeño→menor. Never más bueno for quality — that is a different meaning, about someone being kind.

### task-a2-gestion [task A2] Resuelve tres gestiones
SEQ: keep day=160
canDo: get something done: on the phone, in a shop, and at a desk
summary: These are the conversations that actually go wrong on a trip, and all three have a fixed shape. Learning the shape is worth more than learning more vocabulary.
keywords: Quería… = I'd like… | ¿Podría…? = Could you…? | ¿Cuánto es? = How much is it? | el horario = the opening hours | la cita = the appointment | rellenar = to fill in | el formulario = the form | Gracias por todo. = Thanks for everything.
- What to do: Write three short transactions: one phone call, one shop exchange where something is wrong with what you bought, and one where you ask for information you need.
- The phone shape: ¿Dígame? · say who you are · say what you want · arrange or thank · close. Spanish phone openings are fixed and sound abrupt in translation — that is normal.
- When something is wrong: Es que… introduces the problem, and it is doing real work: it frames what follows as an explanation rather than a complaint. Quería cambiar esto, es que me queda pequeño.

### task-a2-razones [task A2] Explica una decisión
SEQ: move day=139
canDo: give reasons, weigh conditions, and advise someone else
summary: Up to now you have said what is. This unit says why, and what would happen if — which is the point where a conversation stops being an exchange of facts.
keywords: porque = because | como = since, as | por eso = that's why | si = if | Lo mejor es… = The best thing is… | Yo que tú… = If I were you… | Te aconsejo… = I advise you… | depende de = it depends on
- What to do: Explain a real decision you made or are making, in ten to fourteen sentences. Give at least three reasons, and one condition that would have changed it.
- Why, and what for: Porque gives a cause; para gives a purpose. Estudio español porque me gusta versus Estudio español para trabajar en Madrid — learners use porque for both and lose the difference.
- Advise someone: Close by advising someone in the same position: Yo que tú…, Lo mejor es…, Si fuera tú…

### task-a2-sitio [task A2] Lleva a alguien por tu barrio
SEQ: keep day=174
canDo: guide someone through a place and describe what is there
summary: A1 gave you left and right. This is the same skill at conversation length — a route with landmarks, distances and a reason to go.
keywords: dentro / fuera = inside / outside | al fondo = at the back | alrededor = around | enfrente = opposite | a cinco minutos andando = five minutes' walk away | a dos manzanas = two blocks away | justo al lado = right next door | un poco más allá = a bit further on
- What to do: Write a guided walk through somewhere you know well, ten to fourteen sentences. Include three places worth stopping and roughly how long it takes between them.
- Position words: Dentro, fuera, alrededor, al fondo, a lo largo de, enfrente. These are what turn a list of places into a route somebody could follow.
- Distance and time: A cinco minutos andando, a dos manzanas, justo al lado, un poco más allá. Spanish measures short urban distances in minutes far more than in metres.

### task-a2-escribir [task A2] Escribe una reseña y un correo
SEQ: move day=193
canDo: write at paragraph length, not message length
summary: A1 wrote notes and messages. A2 writes something with a middle — a text that has to hold together for three paragraphs rather than three lines.
keywords: Estimado / Querido = Dear (formal / informal) | Un cordial saludo = Kind regards | Un abrazo = Best wishes (warm) | Te escribo para… = I'm writing to… | Estaba comiendo = I was eating | salir corriendo = to run off | seguir hablando = to keep talking | el asunto = the subject
- What to do: Write two things: a short review of something you have seen, eaten or visited, and a semi-formal email that makes a request and explains why.
- Use the gerund: Estaba comiendo, salí corriendo, siguió hablando. The gerund is what lets two things happen at once in one sentence, and it is heavily under-used by learners at this level.
- Register in email: Semi-formal Spanish opens Estimado/a or Buenos días, uses usted throughout, and closes Un cordial saludo. Mixing tú into it halfway through is the commonest mistake.

### task-a2-repaso [task A2] Revisa lo que has escrito en A2
SEQ: keep day=194
canDo: find the agreement, tense and pronoun errors in your own A2 writing
summary: You have written a weekend, a childhood, an anecdote, an interview, a comparison, a decision and a review. This unit explained the machinery under all of it. Now use it on your own work.
keywords: ser / estar = to be (what it is / where or how it is) | por / para = because of / in order to | lo / la / le = it, him, her / to him, to her | el / la / los / las = the | muy / mucho = very / a lot | revisar = to check over | el error = the mistake
- What to do: Open three things you wrote earlier in A2. Read each one three times, looking for one thing only on each pass.
- Pass one — tense: Is every preterite an event and every imperfect a description? A scene set in the preterite is the commonest A2 error, and it makes a story read like a police report.
- Pass two — pronouns: Any noun repeated three times in a paragraph? Any le lo that should be se lo?
- Pass three — agreement: Articles, adjectives, plurals. The A1 check, now on longer texts where it is easier to lose track.

### task-preguntar [task A1] Pregunta cinco cosas
SEQ: move day=32
canDo: ask five real questions and understand the answers
summary: No new material. You have the question words, the rising voice that turns a statement into a question, and the phrases for when you did not catch it. Use all three on one person.
keywords: qué = what | dónde = where | quién = who | cuántos = how many | cómo = how | por qué = why | ¿Y tú? = And you? | No entiendo. = I don't understand. | ¿Puedes repetir? = Can you repeat that?
- What to do: Write a short interview: five questions and five answers. At least one question must use qué, one dónde, one cuántos, and one must be a yes/no question with no question word at all — just the voice.

### task-cortesia [task A1] La misma petición, tres veces
SEQ: merge day=86 target=task-pedir
canDo: ask for the same thing of a friend, a stranger and an official
summary: One request, three rooms. Nothing about the request changes except who you are talking to — and in Spanish that changes the verb ending, the greeting and how much you apologise for asking.
keywords: por favor = please | gracias = thank you | De nada. = You're welcome. | Perdón / Perdone = Excuse me, sorry | Lo siento. = I'm sorry. | señor / señora = Mr, sir / Mrs, madam | buenos días = good morning | Hasta luego. = See you later.
- What to do: Pick one small thing to ask for. Write the same request three times: to a friend, to a stranger in the street, and to someone official behind a desk. Change only the register.
- What has to change: The friend gets tú and no apology. The stranger gets perdone and usted. The official gets both, plus por favor doing real work rather than decoration.

### task-quedar [task A1] Queda con alguien
SEQ: move day=83
canDo: agree a time and a place to meet, in writing
summary: Numbers, the clock and the days of the week only matter when they are doing something. Here they arrange a meeting, which is what they are for.
keywords: quedar = to arrange to meet | ¿Quedamos? = Shall we meet up? | ¿A qué hora? = At what time? | ¿Dónde? = Where? | a las siete = at seven o'clock | mañana = tomorrow | el viernes = on Friday | Vale. = OK. | De acuerdo. = Agreed.
- What to do: Write six to ten messages between you and a friend arranging to meet. You must settle three things: what day, what time, and where.

### task-gustos [task A1] Cinco cosas que te gustan
SEQ: keep day=49
canDo: say what you like and dislike, and react to what someone else likes
summary: The point of this unit is not the list — it is the reaction. Agreeing and disagreeing with a like needs a mí también (me too) and a mí tampoco (me neither), and picking the wrong one reverses your meaning.
keywords: me gusta = I like | me encanta = I love | no me gusta nada = I don't like it at all | odio = I hate | a mí también = me too | a mí tampoco = me neither | ¿Y tú? = And you?
- What to do: Write five things you like and five you do not, then have a partner react to each. Vary the verb: me gusta (I like), me encanta (I love), no me gusta nada (I don't like at all), odio (I hate).
- The trap to get right: If what you are agreeing with is negative, también becomes tampoco. —No me gusta el frío. —A mí tampoco. Saying a mí también there means the opposite of what you meant.
- Singular or plural: Me gusta el café (I like coffee) but me gustan los perros (I like dogs) — the verb agrees with the thing liked, not with you.

### task-cafe [task A1] Pide en un café
SEQ: move day=78
canDo: order food and drink, ask the price and pay
summary: A café transaction is four moves long and almost entirely fixed. Learn it as a shape and you can walk into any bar in Spain on your first day.
keywords: un café con leche = a white coffee | un café solo = a black coffee | una cerveza = a beer | el agua = the water | por favor = please | ¿Algo más? = Anything else? | Nada más, gracias. = That's all, thanks. | ¿Cuánto es? = How much is it? | la cuenta = the bill
- What to do: Write the whole thing, both sides: greeting, order, something extra, the bill. Eight to twelve lines.

### task-describir [task A1] Describe a alguien hasta que lo adivinen
SEQ: keep day=71
canDo: describe a person or an object well enough that someone can identify it
summary: Description is where adjective agreement stops being a rule and starts being audible. Get the ending wrong and a Spanish speaker hears it immediately.
keywords: Es alto / alta. = He/She is tall. | Tiene el pelo largo. = He/She has long hair. | Lleva… = He/She is wearing… | su / sus = his, her | al lado de = next to | la ventana = the window | los zapatos = the shoes | adivinar = to guess
- What to do: Pick a person everybody knows and describe them in six sentences without naming them. Then do the same for an object in the room.

### task-direcciones [task A1] Lleva a alguien hasta allí
SEQ: move day=95
canDo: give and follow directions to a real place
summary: Directions are the first time location words have to be exactly right — getting a la derecha and a la izquierda the wrong way round sends somebody into the wrong street.
keywords: ¿Hay… por aquí? = Is there a… near here? | ¿Dónde está…? = Where is…? | todo recto = straight on | a la derecha / a la izquierda = on the right / on the left | hasta = as far as | enfrente de = opposite | al lado de = next to | el semáforo = the traffic lights | la farmacia = the chemist
- What to do: Write directions from where you are to somewhere nearby, in five or six steps. Then write the same conversation with someone asking you for them.

### task-rutina [task A1] Cuenta un día entero
SEQ: move day=59
canDo: describe your whole day from waking up to going to bed
summary: Reflexive verbs are the spine of a daily routine and the thing that makes it sound Spanish rather than translated. Me levanto, not levanto.
keywords: levantarse = to get up | desayunar = to have breakfast | comer = to eat, to have lunch | acostarse = to go to bed | a las siete = at seven o'clock | siempre = always | a veces = sometimes | casi nunca = almost never | el compañero = the colleague, classmate

### task-pedir [task A1] Pide tres favores
SEQ: keep day=86
canDo: say what you want, what you need and what you have to do — and ask for help
summary: Every one of these is a verb plus an infinitive, which is the cheapest sentence pattern in Spanish and the one that unlocks the most at this stage.
keywords: ¿Puedes…? = Can you…? | ¿Puede…? = Can you…? (usted) | Necesito… = I need… | Tengo que… = I have to… | ayudar = to help | un momento = a moment | por favor = please | Claro. = Of course. | Lo siento. = I'm sorry.
- What to do: Write three short exchanges in which you ask for something. Use a different opening in each: quiero, necesito, ¿puedes...?
- Want, need, must: Quiero is what you want, and to a stranger it is blunt. Necesito is what you need, which sounds more reasonable. Tengo que is what you have no choice about. All three take a plain infinitive after them.

### task-contar [task A1] Une las frases
SEQ: keep day=101
canDo: join short sentences into something that sounds connected rather than chopped up
summary: A beginner who can say ten true things in a row still sounds like a list. Connectors and que are what turn a list into speech.
keywords: y = and | pero = but | porque = because | también = also | que = that, who, which | primero = first | después = then, afterwards | al final = in the end | contar = to tell (a story)
- What to do: Take a short description you have already written in this course and rewrite it, joining sentences with y, pero, porque, también and que. It should get shorter, not longer.

### dlg-presentarse [dialogue A1] Dos personas se conocen
SEQ: keep day=0
canDo: follow a first conversation between two people, and know what each line is doing
summary: Before any grammar: a whole first meeting, start to finish. You are not expected to produce any of it yet — read it, hear the shape, and notice that almost every line is four words or fewer. Everything this unit teaches is somewhere in here.
