## How the words are chosen and placed

**Frequency.** The lists are the OpenSubtitles 2018 Spanish 50k list (speech) and the Leipzig Colombian web 2015 corpus (Colombian writing). Each word form is credited to its lemma:
- verbs through the project engine (`analyzeToken`, `participlesOf`, plus a clitic-stripping index built from the engine's gerunds, infinitives and imperatives);
- nouns and adjectives through the tagger's lemmatiser (plurals, feminines);
- closed-class words to a function lemma (la/los/las → el);
- forms that are still unresolved through a Wiktionary-derived form → lemma map;
- ambiguous forms (creo = creer/crear, trabajo = noun/verb) split in proportion to each lemma's unambiguous evidence (a few EM rounds).

`freqRank` is the rank by the geometric mean of the two corpora's per-million rates, so a word must be common in speech *and* in Colombian writing. This damps the subtitle crime-drama vocabulary. The **teaching order** weights speech more (70:30), so institutional written words (*entidad*, *municipio*, *gestión*) arrive at B1 and later, not A1. Names (capitalised in more than 85% of web uses, or only a proper noun in the dictionary), English words, vulgar words and Spain-only words are filtered out.

**Three tiers.** Each lexeme is in exactly one, decided by one rule:

| Tier | What | In a day's `lexemes`? | Priority | Count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| taught | Content words presented and carded by the compiler | yes, within the day budgets | that day's session | 2,519 |
| closed | Articles, pronouns, prepositions, conjunctions, determiners/quantifiers, numbers, interjections, the tagger's LEXEME_STOP words (*más, muy, ya, también, solo…*) and the verbs a grammar node is about (*ser, estar, haber, ir, gustar*) | no: grammar and chunks teach them | the first chunk/pattern/grammar example that uses them, or its grammar node (possessives, vosotros, voseo); numbers follow the unit themes (0–20 u01, to 100 u05, hundreds u16) | 192 |
| receptive | Words a reader meets in that unit's texts and is expected to understand: transparent cognates, derived forms of taught words (*llegada* < *llegar*), lower-frequency theme words | no | the unit's first Input day | 1,839 |

The gate's legality and reading checks treat a lexeme listed on no day as known from its `priority` (`tools/gate/load.ts`: "nodes no unit schedules fall back to their graph priority"). So receptive and closed words are legal in content from their session on, but they never become SRS cards. Each one's `note` says which tier it is in and why.

**Why three tiers: the targets and the budgets conflict.** The research doc gives cumulative receptive targets of about 1,000–1,500 at A1, 1,500–2,500 at A2, about 3,250 at B1, about 3,750 at B2 and about 4,500 at C1. It also gives day budgets: Input 12–16, Introduce 4–6, Drill 0–4, other days 0. Filled to the top of every budget, the 60 units' 593 days hold at most 2,904 words (A1 654, A2 678, B1 644, B2 472, C1 456). That is about 65% of the C1 target. The research doc resolves this itself: the targets are *receptive* lemmas, and from B2 they read "+ incidental"; C1 is "20–30 deliberate items + incidental tap-to-save from input". So the deliberate tier follows the budgets, and the receptive and closed tiers carry the rest of each band's target.

**Placement, unit by unit, in session order:**
1. **Words a day's nodes need.** A chunk, pattern or grammar node needs the words its id names: *c:tengo-anos* needs *tener* and *año*, *c:trabajo-en* needs *trabajar*. They go on that day, or the latest earlier day with room. (Example sentences inside a node do not count, or the first days would drown.) Placed on or before the day they are needed: all of them.
2. **Input day(s)** take the unit's theme seeds (`curated/units.tsv`, hand-picked LatAm words per unit, most central first). Next come old-deck words of the unit's PCIC areas at about the unit's level.
3. **Introduce and Drill days** take any seeds left over (a full unit's leftovers carry to the next unit first), then the general frequency stream. The stream is content words in teaching order, capped by band: A1 ≤ rank 900, A2 ≤ 1,900, B1 ≤ 3,000, B2 ≤ 3,900. Transparent cognates beyond the top 400 skip the stream and are left for the receptive tier, so card slots go to words a learner could not guess.
4. **Receptive** words fill each band up to its cumulative target. They are theme words and cognates; at A1–A2 only everyday ones (spoken at least a quarter as often as written), and cognates can come up to two bands early because they are read on sight. Each goes to the unit whose PCIC area matches, or round-robin across the band.

**Band floors.** Words the subtitle corpus over-ranks are held back: *matar*, *arma*, *disparar* and *cárcel* to B1; *violar*, *homicidio* and *cadáver* to B2; military ranks to C1. A floored word enters the frequency stream inside its band, not at the front of it.

**How to read it.**
- Davies (2005) puts the top 1,000 Spanish lemmas at 87.8% of spoken text and 76–80% of written text, and the top 3,000 at 94% and 88–90%. Our A1 set (1,050) covers about 82% of subtitle tokens and 68% of web tokens. That is below the raw top-1,000, which includes *Colombia*, *Bogotá* and personal names, and it trails because high-frequency institutional words are deliberately held to B1 and later.
- By the end of B1 the set reaches about 91% of speech and 85% of writing; by C1, 93% and 90%.
- Graded readers want 95–98%. Unit chapters reach that only by keeping to known words plus a few glosses, which is what the gate's reading check (≤2% unknown unglossed) enforces. The receptive tier exists so that check has enough words to work with.

## Tagger: unknown-word rate on the old corpus

`npx tsx tools/tag.ts --all-old` gives the same numbers before and after this work: 2,636 unknown-word tokens out of 196,787 words, and 1,742 low-confidence sentences out of 14,729. **`tag.ts --all-old` never loads the graph**: it tags with the shipped lexicon only. `tools/vocab/measure.ts` reproduces its corpus and tags it again with `lexemes.json` registered:

| Tagger lexicon | Unknown-word tokens | Rate | Low-confidence sentences |
|---|---|---|---|
| as shipped (what `tag.ts --all-old` reports) | 2,636 | 1.34% | 1,742 |
| + lexemes.json, registered as `tools/gate/load.ts` does it (by `es`) | 2,493 | 1.27% | 1,682 |
| + lexemes.json, registered by the id's lemma | 2,183 | 1.11% | 1,522 |

Registering by the id's lemma cuts unknown-word tokens by 17%. Registering the way the gate does today cuts them by only 5%.

**A one-line fix for the tagger and gate code (not made here; tagger and gate code were out of scope).** `tools/gate/load.ts` line 148 registers each lexeme by `n.es ?? <id lemma>`. Following the schema, nouns' `es` carries the article ("la mesa"), and `registerLexemes` skips any lemma containing a space. As a result **no noun in lexemes.json reaches the tagger through the gate**. Registering `n.id.replace(/^l:/, '').replace(/#.*/, '')` instead (the id's lemma) fixes it. `es` stays learner-facing, with the article.

Still unknown after registration by id (top 15): *genuinamente* 25, *documentado* 20, *genuina* 19, *genuino* 18, *incomodidad* 17, *hallazgo* 14, *organizadores* 11, *asimetría* 10, *adaptarse* 9, *replicar* 9, *inquietante* 8, *trimestre* 8, *alegando* 8, *incentivos* 8, *rigor* 7. These are mostly C1 PCIC words outside the 4,550, or inflections the lemmatiser does not fold. The full list is in `tools/vocab/out/measure.json`.

## Decisions to review

- **Lexeme ids follow the tagger.** Pronominal verbs are lexemes of their own (89 of them: `l:levantarse`, `l:irse`, `l:sentirse`, `l:acordarse` …), because the tagger reads "me levanto" as *levantarse*. Their frequency is half their base verb's, since the lists fold them together. Plural-only nouns keep the tagger's lemma and display the plural article: *los padres, las gafas, las ganas, los celos, las vacaciones* (`l:vacación`), *las felicitaciones*.
- **Sense splits** (18, all as `l:<lemma>#<sense>`). The base id keeps the commonest sense, because the tagger only ever emits the base id: *medio#half*, *bajo#prep*, *tiempo#weather*, *llevar#wear*, *tomar#drink*, *quedar#fit*, *seguro#insurance*, *pasado#past*, *cuarto#num*, *pedir#order*, *orden#command*, *carrera#street*, *cuenta#bill*, *banco#bench*, *dulce#candy*, *grado#graduation*, *sobre#envelope*, *mono#blond*.
- **Closed words are lexemes too**, for coverage tracking, and are never carded. The task allowed either way; this is the consistent choice. Three things follow:
  - Adverbs of place and time (*aquí, ayer, hoy, cerca*) are vocabulary and taught.
  - Only the tagger's LEXEME_STOP adverbs (*más, muy, ya, también, todavía, solo, luego, entonces…*) are closed.
  - Theme seeds that turned out to be closed-class (u03:gustar, u09:nunca, u12:entonces, u12:luego, u14:porque, u29:cierto, u42:usted, u42:tú, u42:sumercé, u51:vosotros) are tracked, not taught.
- **Chunks are not duplicated.** A word a chunk teaches whole (*hola, gracias, por favor*) is a closed/interjection lexeme, never carded. A content word inside a chunk (*llamar* in *me llamo*, *año* in *tengo … años*) is taught on or before the chunk's day.
- **Multiword theme items are left to the chunk graph**, because the tagger cannot match multiword lexemes: de repente, centro comercial, hoja de vida, pasado mañana, año nuevo, mala suerte, metro cuadrado, orden del día, mamar gallo, tomar del pelo, doble sentido, espacio cultural. These are candidates for new `c:` nodes.
- **`computadora` and `computador`.** *La computadora* (A1, taught) is the neutral LatAm word. Colombia's *el computador* is C1, receptive.
- **Glosses** come from four sources, in order: `curated/*.tsv` (856 hand-written entries, including every closed word and every Colombian sense), the old deck, `verbs.json`, and Wiktionary's first non-regional, non-archaic sense, cleaned. The taught tier was read through once by hand. The receptive tier was spot-checked and filtered by pattern; some long or technical Wiktionary glosses will remain there.
- **Simulator.** `npm run simulate` runs on the real lexemes (6,120 nodes), so the "injected synthetic lexemes" path is no longer taken. With no bank content yet, its load figures say little about the word budget.
